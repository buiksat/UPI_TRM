\begin{abstract}
Recurrent value evaluators spend a finite number of internal refinement
steps before supporting a policy update. Their computation can remain
transient, so evaluation error must be understood without assuming a latent
fixed point. We study this problem in Unrolled Policy Iteration with Tiny
Recursive Models (UPI--TRM), where a recurrent evaluator guides checker-rewarded
plan edits. Our main idea is to compare the deployed depth-$n$ output with a
deeper but still finite endpoint at depth $m$. The deployed value error is
bounded by the discrepancy between these endpoints plus the reference
endpoint's self-bootstrap Bellman residual divided by $1-\gamma^K$.
Using standard residual and conservative policy-improvement arguments, we
carry this radius through advantage estimation into an exact-mixture
improvement bound with signed evaluation error. We also account for
propagated target-network lag and approximate mixture realization.
For persistent latents, both endpoints are evaluated in one augmented MDP
with a shared frozen deployed recurrent map. An exact three-state
calculation gives a nonvacuous value certificate and a positive CPI lower
bound. The guarantees are conditional on the stated population quantities
at one frozen proposal; learned-task performance remains to be established.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Tiny Recursive Models (TRMs) refine an internal representation before
producing an answer~\citep{TRM2025}. Unrolled Policy Iteration with TRMs
(UPI--TRM) uses this inner computation to evaluate plans in a checker-rewarded
edit MDP: a value head supplies approximate advantages, a candidate policy
chooses edits, and each edit advances the environment. The architectural
mapping is in Appendix~\ref{app:trm_mapping} (Figure~\ref{fig:trm_architecture}).

The evaluator is deployed after $n$ recurrent steps, even when its internal
computation is still changing. Comparing its output $U_n$ with a converged
recurrence requires a latent fixed point and control of the distance to it.
Useful learned computation can instead be transient. Our question is:
\emph{can we bound finite-depth evaluation error without assuming recurrent
convergence?}

\paragraph{A finite reference.}
Fix the policy $\pi$ and compare $U_n$ with the same evaluator at a deeper
finite depth $m>n$. The identity
\[
U_n-V^\pi=(U_n-U_m)+(U_m-V^\pi)
\]
separates sensitivity to additional computation from reference accuracy.
Standard Bellman-residual analysis bounds the latter by
$\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_\infty/(1-\gamma^K)$, using a $K$-step
current-policy backup that self-bootstraps from $U_m$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} gives the full conditions.
A deeper reference helps only when its residual compensates for the endpoint
discrepancy; depth alone does not guarantee a tighter bound.

\paragraph{Two depths in one MDP.}
The Bellman argument treats the endpoints as bounded functions. Recurrence
matters when interpreting them as two computation depths of the same deployed
evaluator. With persistent latents, the depth-$n$ map determines what is
carried into the successor state. Substituting a depth-$m$ transition would
change the MDP. We instead keep the policy and transition kernel fixed and
vary only the endpoint used for evaluation.

\paragraph{From evaluation to improvement.}
The value radius controls advantage error, which enters an exact
probability-mixture CPI bound~\citep{KakadeLangford2002}. Exact centering
removes the base-policy component of evaluation error; signed defects retain
information lost by absolute worst-case bounds. Target-network lag and
approximate deployment are separate corrections.

The contributions are a finite-reference recurrent certificate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}), its advantage/CPI and
target-network connection (Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error}), and persistent-state Markovization
with a shared frozen map (Corollary~\ref{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented}).
An exact finite-MDP calculation and explicit implementation correspondence
complete the analysis. The proof uses established residual and CPI machinery.
All guarantees concern one candidate against one frozen base at a fixed
snapshot; learned-task performance and training convergence are not established.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

TRMs and HRMs use recurrent latent refinement for structured prediction
\citep{TRM2025,HRM2025}; deep equilibrium models use an equilibrium
representation~\citep{Bai2019DEQ}. Here the evaluator is read at finite depth,
with contraction needed only for the optional fixed-point specialization.
Classical approximate dynamic programming controls value error and its
propagation through policy or value updates
\citep{BertsekasTsitsiklis1996,Munos2003API,MunosSzepesvari2008,Scherrer2014}.
Our primary result instead uses a fixed-policy, population sup-norm residual;
Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_lp} states the coverage condition for its
weighted-norm version.

CPI and safe policy iteration control occupancy shift and approximate
improvement~\citep{KakadeLangford2002,Kakade2003Thesis,Pirotta2013SafePI,Metelli2021SafePI}.
We supply the evaluation term for one exact probability mixture from two
finite recurrent endpoints. Learning to search casts structured prediction as
sequential decisions~\citep{Daume2009SEARN,Ross2011DAgger,Chang2015LOLS};
verifiable-reward systems use automatic correctness signals~\citep{DeepSeekR1}.
Our inner depth $n$ is computation within one evaluation, distinct from
planning over transitions~\citep{Tamar2016VIN} or the Bellman horizon $K$.
Appendix~\ref{app:extended_related_work} preserves the extended comparison,
including additional recurrent, control, and constraint-task literature.

\section{Background and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{The outer edit MDP.}
An edit $a_t$ changes plan $y_t$ for a fixed instance $x$. With $h_t$ edits
remaining, the episodic state and nonterminal successor are
\[
s_t=(x,y_t,h_t),\qquad
s_{t+1}=\bigl(x,\mathrm{edit}(y_t,a_t;x),h_t-1\bigr).
\]
Solving, terminal STOP, or budget exhaustion sends the process to the shared
absorber $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$. A bounded checker score $c(x,y)$ supplies
reward, optionally shaped as $r=r_0+\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$ with
$\Phi=c$~\citep{Ng1999Shaping}. We maximize discounted return $\eta(\pi)$
from $\rho$, with $\gamma\in[0,1)$. The shaped boundary is
$V^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b=-C_{\max}$; folding its tail into the terminal
reward with zero terminal bootstrap preserves returns
(Appendices~\ref{app:shaping_convention} and~\ref{app:implementation}).

\paragraph{The inner evaluator.}
A recurrent map refines a latent while the instance and current plan remain
fixed. Starting from $z^{(0)}=z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y)$ in episodic mode,
\[
z^{(k+1)}=\widetilde f_\theta(z^{(k)},y,x),\qquad
U_n(s)=V_\psi(z^{(n)},x,y).
\]
Depth $n$ counts internal computation; the Bellman horizon $K\ge1$ counts
environment transitions. Here $\widetilde f_\theta=\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}\circ
f_\theta$, where $\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}=\Pi_R$ is Euclidean projection
onto a radius-$R>0$ ball, or the identity when disabled. Projection can
establish an invariant latent set, not strict contraction by itself
(Appendix~\ref{app:contractive_specializations}). The implemented head
reads $(z,x,y)$, not $h$, but rewards, termination, and successors need the clock.

\paragraph{Reset or carried latent state.}
Resetting $z$ at each edit makes $(x,y,h)$ Markov. Carrying it requires
$\bar s=(x,y,z,h)$, since different latents can produce different decisions.
The state stores the \emph{pre-unroll} latent; deployment computes
$F_n(x,y,z)=\widetilde f_\theta^{\circ n}(z,y,x)$, uses its output for action
selection, and carries it only on nonterminal transitions. Both CPI policies
share this frozen map. Figure~\ref{fig:persistent_depth_lanes} separates
comparison from deployment: $\widetilde U_m$ starts from stored $z$ without
changing $F_n$, action sampling, or successors.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/persistent_depth_lanes.tex}}
\caption{A deeper comparison is not a deeper deployment. Both lanes start
from the same stored pre-unroll state. The upper lane uses $F_n$ to choose
an action and carries its output only after a nonterminal transition. The

\label{fig:persistent_depth_lanes}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Values and domains.}
Fix the MDP, recurrence, initialization, value head, and current/candidate
policies. State/action spaces are measurable, policies are stochastic
kernels, and values are bounded measurable functions. On $\mathcal C$,
$\|f\|_\infty=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C}|f(s)|$ is a pointwise supremum, not a
batch maximum or implicit essential supremum. Every endpoint equals $b$ at
the absorber; latent paths are defined only elsewhere.
Section~\ref{sec:error_bounds} introduces the advantage-evaluation domain;
Appendix~\ref{app:adv_closure_full} gives all domain definitions.

\section{UPI--TRM Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:upi} freezes the base edit policy and recurrent map before
collection. Checker rewards and $K$-step targets fit the value head;
$U_n$-derived centered advantages fit one candidate policy head. The proposal
mixes action probabilities: $\pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$.
Optimizer steps fit this proposal without replacing the base.

The value-derived estimator is
\[
\widehat Q_{U_n}(s,a)=\E[r+\gamma U_n(S')\mid s,a],\qquad
\widehat A_{U_n}(s,a)=\widehat Q_{U_n}(s,a)
-\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}\widehat Q_{U_n}(s,b).
\]
For persistent latents, condition on the full $\bar s$. The theorems analyze
the fitted heads at one frozen snapshot. Approximating the mixture by a
single network requires the separate deployment correction in
Section~\ref{sec:persistent_deployment_main}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\small
\caption{One fixed-base UPI--TRM proposal on the clock-complete edit MDP}
\label{alg:upi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} sample $x$, initialize $y_0$, choose $T\ge1$, set remaining budget $h_0=T$, and initialize $z_0$ for persistent-$z$
\FOR{$t=0,\dots,T-1$}
  \STATE Set $s_t=(x,y_t,h_t)$; for persistent-$z$, set $\bar s_t=(x,y_t,z_t,h_t)$
  \STATE \textbf{Latent input:} episodic-$z$ uses $z_t\gets z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y_t)$; persistent-$z$ uses the carried pre-unroll $z_t$
  \STATE \textbf{Evaluator:} $z_t^{(n)} \gets \widetilde f_\theta^{\circ n}(z_t,y_t,x)$
  \STATE \textbf{Value:} $U_n(s_t)\gets V_\psi(z_t^{(n)},x,y_t)$ in episodic-$z$; $\widetilde U_n(\bar s_t)\gets V_\psi(z_t^{(n)},x,y_t)$ in persistent-$z$
  \STATE \textbf{Action:} sample $a_t\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid y_t,z_t^{(n)},x)$ and apply the edit; observe $r_t$, $y_{t+1}$, and terminal flag $d_t$; set $h_{t+1}=h_t-1$
  \IF{$d_t$ or $h_{t+1}=0$}
    \STATE Store $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{\mathrm{abs}})$ in episodic-$z$, or $(\bar s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{\mathrm{abs}})$ in persistent-$z$
    \STATE \textbf{break}
  \ELSE
    \STATE Set $s_{t+1}=(x,y_{t+1},h_{t+1})$ and $\bar s_{t+1}=(x,y_{t+1},z_t^{(n)},h_{t+1})$
    \STATE Store $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$ in episodic-$z$, or $(\bar s_t,a_t,r_t,\bar s_{t+1})$ in persistent-$z$
    \STATE For persistent-$z$, carry the post-unroll latent: $z_{t+1}\gets z_t^{(n)}$
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Value fit:} form $G^{(K)}$ and regress the mode-appropriate evaluator ($U_n$ or $\widetilde U_n$)
\STATE \textbf{Advantage estimate:} compute $\widehat A$ centered conditional on $s_t$, or on full $\bar s_t$ in persistent-$z$
\STATE \textbf{Candidate fit:} take policy-gradient steps to obtain one $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$ while the base policy and recurrent map remain frozen
\STATE \textbf{Mixture construction:} $\pi_{\alpha} \gets (1-\alpha)\pi_\phi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$
\STATE \textbf{Optional deployment heuristic:} distill $\pi_{\alpha}$ into a single policy network
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Only nonterminal transitions carry a latent; every terminal cause ends
collection after its final record. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} gives
folded targets and replay details. Exact baseline enumeration ensures
statewise centering; approximate integration instead contributes a signed
defect (Table~\ref{tab:centering_regimes}, Appendix~\ref{app:adv_bounds}).
One depth-$n$ unroll per action term over $T$ states costs
$O(T|\mathcal A|n)$ recurrent-map applications. Interpolating weights,
logits, or hidden states, or distilling the mixture, generally changes its law.

\section{Finite-Reference Evaluation and Advantage Error}
\label{sec:error_bounds}

We first use episodic notation; Section~\ref{sec:persistent_deployment_main}
applies the same argument on the fixed augmented MDP.

\subsection{The finite-reference value certificate}
\label{sec:value_error_bounds}
The triangle inequality splits $\|U_n-V^\pi\|_\infty$ into endpoint
discrepancy and reference error. Bellman contraction controls the latter
through its self-bootstrap residual~\citep{Puterman1994,SuttonBarto2018}.

For later advantage estimation, let $\mathcal C_\pi$ carry the initial law
and be invariant under $\pi$. Write
$\mathcal C=\mathcal R^{\mathrm{adv}}_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}$ for a
measurable set containing $\mathcal C_\pi$, the absorber, and successors of
one current or candidate action, followed by any current-policy continuation
(Definition~\ref{def:adv_closure}). Define
$\mathcal C^\circ=\mathcal C\setminus\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$ and
$\|f\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C}|f(s)|$. On this invariant domain the residual bound
is
\begin{equation}
\|V - V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma^K}\,
\|V-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K V\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}},
\label{eq:boundA}
\end{equation}
Apply this estimate to $V=U_m$ and add the endpoint discrepancy:

\begin{theorem}[Measurable finite-reference-depth value decomposition]
\label{thm:finite_reference_depth}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a measurable state set with its trace $\sigma$-algebra,
let $P_\pi$ be a Markov kernel satisfying
$P_\pi(\mathcal C\mid s)=1$ for every $s\in\mathcal C$, and fix
$\gamma\in[0,1)$ and an integer $K\ge1$. If an initial law is used, assume
$\rho(\mathcal C)=1$. Assume the one-step reward is uniformly bounded and
its fixed-policy conditional mean
$r^\pi(s):=\E_s^\pi[r_0]$ is measurable on $\mathcal C$. Then the $K$-step
expected reward
\begin{equation}
r_K^\pi(s)
:=
\E_s^\pi\!\left[\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\gamma^i r_i\right]
\label{eq:rK_definition}
\end{equation}
is bounded and measurable on $\mathcal C$. On the Banach space
$B_b(\mathcal C)$ of bounded measurable functions with the ordinary
pointwise sup norm, define
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal T_K^\pi V)(s)
:=
r_K^\pi(s)+\gamma^K(P_\pi^K V)(s).
\label{eq:TK_measurable}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal T_K^\pi:B_b(\mathcal C)\to B_b(\mathcal C)$ is a
$\gamma^K$-contraction and has a unique fixed point $V^\pi$. For arbitrary
endpoint functions $U_n,U_m\in B_b(\mathcal C)$ and finite integers
$0\le n<m$,
\begin{align}
\|U_n - V^\pi\|_\infty
&\le
\underbrace{\|U_n-U_m\|_\infty}_{\text{finite-depth discrepancy}}
+
\underbrace{\frac{\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_\infty}{1-\gamma^K}}_{\text{reference-depth residual}}.
\label{eq:finite_reference_depth}
\end{align}

For the recurrent specialization, take
$\mathcal C=\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ and
$\mathcal C^\circ=\mathcal C\setminus\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$. On
$\mathcal C^\circ$, fix a jointly measurable recurrent map
$(s,z)\mapsto T(s,z)$, a common measurable initialization into its declared
latent domain, and a latent norm, and define $z^{(j)}$ by iterating that map
and $U_j(s)=V_\psi(z^{(j)}(s),x(s),y(s))$. At the absorber define only the
common scalar boundary $U_j(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b$, and assume the two endpoint
functions lie in $B_b(\mathcal C)$. If the measurable scalar head is
$L_V$-Lipschitz in its latent argument uniformly while $(x,y)$ is held fixed,
then
\begin{align}
\|U_n-U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le
L_V\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\|z^{(n)}(s)-z^{(m)}(s)\|
\label{eq:finite_endpoint_discrepancy}\\
&\le
L_V\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}
\|z^{(j+1)}(s)-z^{(j)}(s)\|
\label{eq:finite_path_length_exact}\\
&\le
L_V\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\|z^{(j+1)}(s)-z^{(j)}(s)\|.
\label{eq:finite_path_length}
\end{align}
If $\mathcal C^\circ$ is empty, the nonnegative latent suprema in this
chain are defined to be zero.
\end{theorem}

The discrepancy measures sensitivity to additional computation; the residual
measures agreement with the frozen policy's value equation. A stable output
can still have a large residual. Both endpoints remain finite, and the backup
uses the same policy and kernel as $V^\pi$. The Lipschitz clause converts
latent separation to value separation. Taking the supremum after the path
sum is sharper than summing separate suprema. Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

Appendix~\ref{app:finite_endpoint_remarks} gives the noncontractive examples, additional endpoint bounds, and frozen-schedule extension.


Uniform recurrent contraction gives the optional geometric $L_z^n$ limit
in Appendix~\ref{app:contractive_specializations}. An alternative
weighted-$L_p$ certificate appears in Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_lp}; it
requires explicit domination of the fixed-policy occupancy by the reference
measure. Neither specialization is needed for the finite-endpoint result.

\subsection{From value error to candidate advantage error}

The action value and baseline each contribute at most $\gamma E$ from
successor value error $E$, giving $2\gamma E$. Policy overlap sharpens the
candidate-specific bound. Define
\begin{equation}
\tau(s):=\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s),\pi(\cdot\mid s)\right),
\qquad
\tau_\infty:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_\pi}\tau(s)\le1.
\label{eq:policy_overlap_def}
\end{equation}
We use $b(s)=\E_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}[\widehat A(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)]$ for
the signed candidate defect, and $\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_\pi}|b(s)|$. The function $b(s)$ is distinct from the
scalar absorbing boundary $b$. The normalized discounted occupancy is
$d_\pi=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t\rho P_\pi^t$.

Value error is controlled on $\mathcal C$, advantage error on $\mathcal C_\pi$.
The check is post-candidate because $\mathcal C$ contains candidate successors.

\begin{corollary}[Advantage-error bridge]
\label{cor:epsA_dials}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}, define
\begin{equation}
B_{n,m}
:=
\|U_n-U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+
\frac{\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:Bnm_def}
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat A=\widehat A_{U_n}$ be the exact one-step centered estimator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}, with the transition expectation evaluated exactly (or applied to a deterministic transition) and the baseline computed exactly under $\pi$. Then Assumption~\ref{ass:Ahat_error_bound} holds with the valid choice
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_A = 2\gamma B_{n,m}.
\label{eq:epsA_finite_reference}
\end{equation}
If Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} holds on
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and the $L_V$-Lipschitz head condition
also holds there, sending $m\to\infty$ gives the fixed-point specialization
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_A
&\le
\underbrace{\frac{2\gamma\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*}{1-\gamma^K}}_{\text{architectural}}
+
\underbrace{2\gamma\,L_V\,\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,C_z}_{\text{unrolling}}.
\label{eq:epsA_dials}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

The reference assesses the depth-$n$ estimator without changing its action
selection. Exact centering gives the sharper candidate-average bound:

\begin{corollary}[Candidate evaluation bias from policy overlap]
\label{cor:candidate_overlap}
Let $\widehat A_V$ be the exactly centered one-step estimator,
$e=V-V^\pi$, and
$\delta_V=\|e\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$.
Use $\tau$ and $\tau_\infty$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy_overlap_def}.
Then, for every $s\in\mathcal C_\pi$,
\begin{equation}
\left|
\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}
[\widehat A_V(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)]
\right|
\le 2\gamma\delta_V\tau(s),
\label{eq:candidate_overlap_pointwise}
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
&\le2\gamma\delta_V\tau_\infty,
\label{eq:candidate_overlap_uniform}\\
\E_{s\sim d_\pi}|b(s)|
&\le2\gamma\delta_V\E_{s\sim d_\pi}\tau(s).
\label{eq:candidate_overlap_average}
\end{align}
For an exactly centered $K$-step estimator whose intervening actions follow
$\pi$, replace $\gamma$ by $\gamma^K$. The fallback
$2\gamma\delta_V$ follows from $\tau\le1$.
\end{corollary}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_candidate_overlap}.

Exact centering cancels the shared part of the two action laws. Consequently,
$2\gamma\delta_V\tau(s)$ can be much smaller than the worst-case
$2\gamma\delta_V$, and is zero when the policies agree. Section~\ref{sec:cpi}
uses this candidate bias in an improvement bound. In particular, under the
exact-mixture and centering hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error},
the finite-reference radius gives
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
\ge
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}B_{n,m}
-\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:finite_reference_cpi}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat L_\pi$ is the estimated CPI surrogate and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}$ bounds the magnitude of the candidate's expected
true advantage on the mixture domain; both are defined in
Section~\ref{sec:cpi}. This display completes the link from finite recurrent
endpoints to policy improvement. For centered $K$-step estimators the
advantage factor becomes $2\gamma^K$; other estimators, including GAE,
need their own error bound (Remark~\ref{rem:A_error_kstep_app}).

\section{Target-Network Population-Residual Bridge}
\label{sec:contraction}

Value regression fits $U_n$ to a target network $\bar V$, whereas the
certificate needs a backup of the evaluated function itself. For a frozen
current-policy trajectory, the target is
$G^{(K)}=\sum_{k<K}\gamma^k r_k+\gamma^K\bar V(S_K)$, with loss
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{val}}=\E_{S_0\sim\mu}[(U_n(S_0)-\sg(G^{(K)}))^2]$.
Here $\mu$ selects starting states; the conditional trajectory law is $\pi$.
Even zero target residual can leave a self-bootstrap residual when the
networks differ. The correction is propagated target lag:

\begin{proposition}[Target-network population residual bridge]
\label{prop:target_network_bridge}
Let $\mathcal E$ be a measurable $P_\pi$-invariant state domain. Assume
$r_K^\pi\in B_b(\mathcal E)$ and
$U_q,\bar V\in B_b(\mathcal E)$ for a finite depth $q\ge0$, and suppose the conditional $K$-step target
trajectory follows the frozen policy $\pi$. Define
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
:=
\left\|U_q-
\left(r_K^\pi+\gamma^K P_\pi^K\bar V\right)
\right\|_{\infty,\mathcal E}.
\label{eq:target_population_residual}
\end{equation}
Retain the propagated target lag by defining
\begin{align}
R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
&:=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
+\gamma^K\|P_\pi^K(\bar V-U_q)\|_{\infty,\mathcal E},
\label{eq:R_targ_propagated}\\
R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{\sup}
&:=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
+\gamma^K\|\bar V-U_q\|_{\infty,\mathcal E}.
\label{eq:R_targ_sup}
\end{align}
Then the self-bootstrap residual satisfies
\begin{align}
\|U_q-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_q\|_{\infty,\mathcal E}
&\le
R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
\le R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{\sup}.
\label{eq:target_network_residual_bridge}
\end{align}
For the formal absorbing representation, require
$U_q(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=\bar V(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b$ and use the same
absorbing reward and boundary in both population operators. The equivalent
folded implementation may instead fold $\gamma b$ into the
terminal-transition reward and mask the terminal bootstrap to zero, but it
must not add the absorbing tail again.
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_target_network_bridge}.

Propagated lag averages before taking the supremum; the ordinary sup-lag is
a possibly looser fallback. Both vanish when the functions coincide.
Table~\ref{tab:residual_composition} (Appendix~\ref{app:residual_choices})
and Appendix~\ref{app:proof_finite_depth_certificate} give the value/CPI
substitutions. These are population sup norms: even population mean-square
regression controls only an $L^2(\mu)$ conditional residual. Empirical loss
or a finite-batch maximum establishes neither the uniform residual nor
uniform propagated lag.

\section{Conservative Policy Improvement}
\label{sec:cpi}

The exact mixture $\pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$
can select its base or candidate component with a Bernoulli draw at each
decision. CPI corrects the base-policy surrogate for advantage error and
occupancy shift~\citep{KakadeLangford2002}; we keep signed error and bound
shift using this selector. Error uses $\mathcal C_\pi$; occupancy needs
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ to contain full trajectories under both policies,
not just one deviation (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_closure_full},
Table~\ref{tab:domain_map}). The uniform-error assumption below suffices
for the scalar specialization but is not needed for the signed bound.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform advantage error on $\mathcal C_\pi$]
\label{ass:Ahat_error_bound}
For some $\varepsilon_A\ge 0$:
\begin{equation}
|\widehat{A}(s,a) - A^\pi(s,a)| \le \varepsilon_A
\end{equation}
for every $s\in\mathcal C_\pi$ and for $\pi(da\mid s)$- and
$\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(da\mid s)$-almost every $a$.
\end{assumption}

Write
$d_\pi=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t\rho P_\pi^t$ and
define the true and estimated CPI surrogates
\begin{align*}
L_\pi(\pi')
&:=\eta(\pi)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\pi,\,a\sim\pi'(\cdot\mid s)}[A^\pi(s,a)],\\
\widehat L_\pi(\pi')
&:=\eta(\pi)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\pi,\,a\sim\pi'(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A(s,a)].
\end{align*}

\begin{lemma}[Exact-mixture occupancy]
\label{lem:exact_mixture_occupancy}
Let $\pi$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$ act in the same MDP from the same initial
law, and let $\pi_\alpha$ be their exact pointwise probability mixture. For
$\alpha\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{TV}(d_{\pi_\alpha},d_\pi)
\le
\frac{\gamma\alpha}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}.
\label{eq:exact_mixture_occupancy_tv}
\end{equation}
For finite or countable state spaces, with
$\operatorname{TV}(p,q)=\tfrac12\|p-q\|_1$,
\begin{equation}
\|d_{\pi_\alpha}-d_\pi\|_1
\le
\frac{2\gamma\alpha}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}.
\label{eq:exact_mixture_occupancy_l1}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_mixture_occupancy}.

The order-$\alpha$ occupancy shift multiplies the candidate's
order-$\alpha$ surrogate contribution. The resulting correction is
$O(\alpha^2)$ for a fixed domain and advantage span. The evaluation
correction combines base-policy centering defect $c(s)$ and candidate bias
$b(s)$ under $d_\pi$, the surrogate's occupancy.

\begin{theorem}[Occupancy-aware CPI with signed evaluation error]
\label{thm:monotone_with_error}
Fix one discounted MDP with bounded rewards and measurable current and
candidate policy kernels. Let $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and let
$\pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$ be their exact
pointwise probability-space mixture, with no parameter interpolation,
hidden-state interpolation, logit interpolation, or distillation. Let
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ be measurable with
$\rho(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}})=1$ and invariant under both $\pi$ and
$\pi_\alpha$, as in Definition~\ref{def:mixture_deployment_domains}. Assume
that a measurable $\pi$-invariant domain
$\mathcal C_\pi\subseteq\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ also carries $\rho$.
Assume the functions below are bounded and measurable on their declared
domains. Define $g$ on $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and $c,b$ on
$\mathcal C_\pi$ by
\begin{align}
g(s)
&:=\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}[A^\pi(s,a)],
&
\Delta_g
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}}g(s)
-\inf_{s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}}g(s),
\label{eq:g_span_def}\\
c(s)
&:=\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A(s,a)],
&
b(s)
&:=\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}
[\widehat A(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)],
\label{eq:signed_defects}\\
\Xi_\alpha
&:=\E_{s\sim d_\pi}[(1-\alpha)c(s)+\alpha b(s)].
\label{eq:Xi_alpha}
\end{align}
Then the surrogate error is exactly
\begin{equation}
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)-L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
=\frac{\Xi_\alpha}{1-\gamma},
\label{eq:surrogate_signed_identity}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
\ge
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-\frac{\Xi_\alpha}{1-\gamma}
-\frac{\gamma\alpha^2\Delta_g}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:main_theorem_occupancy}
\end{equation}
Because $\Xi_\alpha$ is signed, a usable one-sided guarantee may replace it
by any established $B_\alpha\ge\Xi_\alpha$, for example
\begin{equation}
B_\alpha
=(1-\alpha)\E_{d_\pi}|c(s)|
+\alpha\E_{d_\pi}|b(s)|.
\label{eq:Balpha_abs}
\end{equation}

Under exact statewise centering,
\begin{equation}
\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A(s,a)] = 0
\qquad\text{for every }s\in\mathcal C_\pi,
\label{eq:centering_condition}
\end{equation}
define the uniform candidate-policy bias
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
\triangleq
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_\pi}
\left|
\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}\!\left[\widehat{A}(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)\right]
\right|.
\label{eq:epsA0_def}
\end{equation}
If $\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}<\infty$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}:=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}}|g(s)|<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
\ge
\widehat{L}_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-
\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}\,\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
-
\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)},
\label{eq:main_theorem}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Exact centering leaves candidate bias scaled by $\alpha/(1-\gamma)$.
Occupancy shift uses $\Delta_g$ and the exact denominator
$1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha$. A negative defect credits underestimated advantage;
an absolute bound loses that credit (Section~\ref{sec:finite_mdp_example}).
Any established $B_\alpha\ge\Xi_\alpha$ gives a one-sided guarantee.
Sampled/learned baselines contribute $c(s)$, even with a small batch mean.
Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:cpi_derivation}.

The domain and span may depend on $\alpha$. A common-domain safe-step
extension appears in Corollary~\ref{cor:one_step_improvement_condition}
(Appendix~\ref{app:safe_step_statement}); Appendix~\ref{app:safe_step_boundaries}
gives its proof and boundaries. Assumption~\ref{ass:Ahat_error_bound} implies
$\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}\le\varepsilon_A$, but the signed theorem
does not require it.

\section{Persistent State and Deployment Realization}
\label{sec:persistent_deployment_main}

\paragraph{Markovization with a carried latent.}
On $\bar s=(x,y,z,h)$, $F_n$ determines both policies' post-unroll inputs and
the nonterminal successor $(x,\mathrm{edit}(y,a;x),F_n(x,y,z),h-1)$.
Termination reaches the common absorber without carrying a latent. Keeping
this frozen map while evaluating $\widetilde U_m$ from the same stored $z$
gives bounded endpoints in one MDP, so Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}
yields
\begin{equation}
\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_\infty
\le
\|\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_m\|_\infty
+\frac{\|\widetilde U_m-
\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_m\|_\infty}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_finite_reference_main}
\end{equation}
The norm includes the common scalar boundary on the augmented
advantage-evaluation domain. Corollary~\ref{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented}
gives the full conditions and target/CPI forms. Slow drift is required only
for the optional memoryless fixed-point comparison.

\paragraph{Approximating the mixture at deployment.}
Distillation or parameter/logit/hidden-state interpolation generally gives
a different law $\widetilde\pi$. On a domain invariant under both policies,
let its uniform TV discrepancy from $\pi_\alpha$ be at most
$\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}$, and let $\Delta_r$ be the common reward-interval
width. Under the standard-Borel and measurable-kernel assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation},
\begin{equation}
|\eta(\widetilde\pi)-\eta(\pi_\alpha)|
\le
\frac{\Delta_r\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})}.
\label{eq:deployment_summary_main}
\end{equation}
The first-mismatch penalty vanishes for exact realization. Either-direction
uniform KL control supplies a TV radius (Corollary~\ref{cor:deployment_pinsker}).
Persistent policies must share the recurrent transition map; changing it
requires a different-MDP analysis.

\section{Exact Finite-MDP Nonvacuity Check}
\label{sec:finite_mdp_example}

Exact enumeration gives a nonvacuous value radius and signed CPI bound,
but a vacuous absolute specialization. Nothing is sampled or learned.

Consider two nonabsorbing states and a shared absorber,
$\mathcal S=\{s_0,s_1,s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$, actions $\{a,b\}$, initial law
$\rho=\delta_{s_0}$, $\gamma=1/2$, and $K=1$. The current policy always
chooses $a$ and the candidate always chooses $b$. Deterministic
reward-successor pairs are
\[
(s_0,a):(0,s_0),\quad (s_0,b):(1,s_1),\quad
(s_1,a):(0,s_0),\quad (s_1,b):(0,s_1),
\]
and both absorber actions return $(0,s_{\mathrm{abs}})$.
The complete domains are
$\mathcal C_\pi=\{s_0,s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$ and
$\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}
=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}=\mathcal S$.
For the scalar recurrence, set $z^{(0)}=0$,
$T_s(z)=u_s-z/2$, $(u_{s_0},u_{s_1})=(1/4,-1/4)$, and
$u_{s_{\mathrm{abs}}}=0$, with $V_\psi(z,s)=z$. Use deployed depth $n=1$, comparison depth $m=2$, and
$\alpha=1/2$. Table~\ref{tab:finite_mdp_summary} reports the exact result.

it uses two finite endpoints rather than a recurrent limit.

The policy calculation illustrates why signs and spans matter. The estimator
understates candidate advantage: $b(s_0)=-1/4$ and
$\Xi_{1/2}=-1/8$. Correcting the surrogate $3/4$ therefore \emph{adds}
$1/4$. The exact-span occupancy penalty is $1/3$, so
\[
\eta(\pi_{1/2})\ge\frac34+\frac14-\frac13=\frac23,
\qquad \eta(\pi_{1/2})=\frac34.
\]
The absolute uniform specialization instead subtracts $1/4$ for evaluation
error and replaces the span $\Delta_g=1$ by
$2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}=2$, doubling the occupancy penalty:
\[
\frac34-\frac14-\frac23=-\frac16.
\]
Both bounds are valid, but only the signed-span version certifies improvement
over $\eta(\pi)=0$. This is an exact nonvacuity check, not a learned-model
performance result. Appendix~\ref{app:finite_mdp_derivation} derives every
quantity; \texttt{verify\_finite\_mdp.py} reproduces the rational calculation.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A finite reference separates endpoint discrepancy from self-bootstrap residual
and supplies a value radius for one-proposal CPI. Target lag and deployment
mismatch require separate corrections; persistent state requires the clock
and a shared frozen map. Recurrent convergence is unnecessary.

These conditional guarantees establish neither optimizer/policy-sequence
convergence, finite-sample coverage, near-optimality, nor learned-task
improvement. The exact MDP checks nonvacuity. Certification still requires
uniform residual, centering/defect, and deployment controls; finite-population
diagnostics do not establish them. Appendix~\ref{app:extended_discussion}
gives the extended interpretation and proposed certification program.
Ethics, reproducibility, and AI-use statements follow.
\label{sec:main_text_end}

\section{Optional Recurrent-Contraction Specializations}
\label{app:contractive_specializations}

This appendix adds structure to the finite-reference result. Uniform
contraction on an invariant latent set gives a recurrent fixed point and
turns the finite path into a geometric tail. The primary theorem needs
neither this limit nor the following contraction assumption.

Projection onto $B_R$ is nonexpansive and establishes invariance when the
initializer lies in that ball. Strict contraction additionally requires a
proved modulus below one for the complete projected map. Operator-norm
clamping or local finite differences may guide an intervention, but their
use alone is not such a proof. Projection-disabled mode uses the identity;
setting $R=0$ would instead collapse every latent to zero.

\begin{assumption}[Domain-relative forward-invariant contraction]
\label{ass:Lip}
Fix a declared nonabsorbing state domain $\mathcal D^\circ$. The latent
space $\ZZ$ is a complete normed space (in our implementation,
$\mathbb R^d$ with the Euclidean norm). For $s\in\mathcal D^\circ$, set
\[
T_s(z):=\widetilde f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s)).
\]
There exist a closed set $\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\subseteq\ZZ$ and
$L_z\in[0,1)$ such that, uniformly for every $s\in\mathcal D^\circ$,
\[
T_s(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}})\subseteq\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}},
\qquad
\|T_s(z)-T_s(z')\|\le L_z\|z-z'\|
\quad(z,z'\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}).
\]
Every initialization used by the result satisfies
$z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ for every
$s\in\mathcal D^\circ$.
\end{assumption}

For the episodic finite-depth and projection-aware specializations, invoke
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} with $\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and assume
the finite $C_z$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Cz_def}. The persistent slow-drift
specialization declares its augmented domain in
Appendix~\ref{sec:twotimescale_persistent}. A global contraction and
initialization condition over every input pair is sufficient, but it is not
implied by a closure-restricted premise. We use $L_z^0=1$ for every
$L_z\in[0,1)$ and $L_z^n=0$ for every integer $n\ge1$ when $L_z=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Domain-relative fixed point and finite-unrolling bias (Banach)]
\label{prop:banach}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip}, for every $s\in\mathcal D^\circ$ the map
$T_s$ has a unique fixed point $z^*(s)\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$. For
every $z^{(0)}(s)\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$, its iterates converge
linearly:
\begin{equation}
\norm{z^{(t)}(s) - z^*(s)}
\;\le\;
L_z^{\,t}\,\norm{z^{(0)}(s) - z^*(s)}.
\label{eq:linear_convergence}
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$, $C_z$ is finite as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Cz_def}, and
$z^{(0)}(s)=z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))$, then uniformly on
$\mathcal C^\circ$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{z^{(n)}(s)-z^*(s)}
\;\le\;
\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,C_z.
\label{eq:finite_unrolling_bias}
\end{equation}
Thus fixed-point existence is asserted only for input pairs represented by
the declared domain. Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip}'s metric contraction and
invariance clauses alone do not imply measurability of $s\mapsto z^*(s)$.

For a sufficient measurability condition, equip the closed set
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra and
$\mathcal D^\circ$ with its declared $\sigma$-algebra. If
\begin{equation}
T:\mathcal D^\circ\times\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}
\longrightarrow\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}},
\qquad T(s,z)=T_s(z),
\label{eq:joint_measurable_recurrence}
\end{equation}
is jointly measurable and
$z^{(0)}:\mathcal D^\circ\to\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ is measurable, then
every iterate $z^{(j)}$ and the fixed-point map $z^*$ are measurable.
\end{proposition}

For any result below that invokes $L_V$ on a declared nonabsorbing domain
$\mathcal D^\circ$, let $\mathcal Z_{\mathcal D}(s)$ be the set of latent
arguments compared by that result at $s$. Assume the scalar head is uniformly
Lipschitz while the instance and plan are held fixed: for every
$s\in\mathcal D^\circ$ and all $z,z'\in\mathcal Z_{\mathcal D}(s)$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|V_\psi(z,x(s),y(s))-V_\psi(z',x(s),y(s))\bigr|
\;\le\;
L_V\,\norm{z-z'}.
\label{eq:LV_def}
\end{equation}
For a contractive specialization one may take
$\mathcal Z_{\mathcal D}(s)=\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$; a pathwise result
needs the condition only at the latent pairs it displays.

\begin{assumption}[Domain-explicit bounded value heads]
\label{ass:bounded_value_head}
For an invocation on a declared nonabsorbing state domain
$\mathcal D^\circ$, declare a latent set
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{bd}}\subseteq\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$. It is either
all of $\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ or an explicitly identified smaller set
containing every latent used by that result. There exists
$V_{\max}<\infty$ such that, for every represented
$s\in\mathcal D^\circ$ and every $z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{bd}}$,
\begin{equation}
|V_\psi(z,x(s),y(s))|\le V_{\max},
\qquad
|V_{\bar\psi}(z,x(s),y(s))|\le V_{\max}.
\label{eq:bounded_value_heads_domain}
\end{equation}
The absorber is handled by its separately declared finite scalar boundary;
no absorbing latent is introduced. This assumption can supply boundedness in
the optional contractive specializations, but it does not replace any
explicit $B_b(\mathcal C)$ premise in a Bellman theorem.
\end{assumption}

For the optional contractive specialization, invoke
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} with $\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and the
finite constant $C_z$ above. For each represented $s\in\mathcal C^\circ$,
write $z^*(s)$ for the unique fixed point of $T_s$. The joint measurability
and measurable initialization make $s\mapsto z^*(s)$ measurable by
Proposition~\ref{prop:banach}. Invoke
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded_value_head} on
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ with
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{bd}}=\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$; together with the
separate finite absorbing boundary, this makes $U_*$ bounded on the closure.
Set $U_*(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=-C_{\max}$ and define
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^* \triangleq
\|U_* - \mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_*\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:eps_res_def}
\end{equation}
Consecutive latent differences satisfy
$\|z^{(j+1)}-z^{(j)}\|\le L_z^j C_z$ uniformly. Hence
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} gives
\begin{align}
\|U_n - V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le
L_V C_z\frac{L_z^n(1-L_z^{m-n})}{1-L_z}
+\frac{\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:finite_reference_contracting}
\end{align}
Uniform convergence $U_m\to U_*$ and Bellman contraction imply convergence
of the residual norm. Taking $m\to\infty$ recovers
\begin{align}
\|U_n - V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\underbrace{\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*}{1-\gamma^K}}_{\text{fixed-point residual}}
+
\underbrace{L_V\,\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,C_z}_{\text{geometric path bound}}.
\label{eq:epsV_dials}
\end{align}
This limit is a specialization, not an assertion that arbitrary TRMs are
designed to converge. Under $\|z_{\mathrm{init}}\|_2\le R$ with
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}=B_R$, projection gives $C_z\le2R$
(Remark~\ref{rem:finite_R_Cz}).

When every represented pre-projection output satisfies
$\|f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s))\|_2\ge\rho_R>R$ for $z\in B_R$, radial projection
reduces the pre-projection Lipschitz upper bound by $R/\rho_R$. The projected
map is contractive only when $(R/\rho_R)L_z^{\mathrm{pre}}(R)<1$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:proj_sat_contraction} gives
\begin{equation}
L_z\;\le\;\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)
\;:=\;\frac{R}{\rho_R}\,L_z^{\mathrm{pre}}(R),
\label{eq:Lz_sat}
\end{equation}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:proj_residual_anchor} gives, for
$B_0(x,y):=V_\psi(0,x,y)$ and
$B_0(s_{\mathrm{abs}}):=-C_{\max}$,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*\;\le\;\varepsilon_0
+\;(1+\gamma^K)\,L_V(R)\,R,
\label{eq:eps_res_proj}
\end{equation}
where
$\varepsilon_0:=\|B_0-\mathcal{T}^\pi_KB_0\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$.
Finite-batch measurements remain diagnostics; they certify neither the
uniform saturation and Lipschitz assumptions nor the required sup-norm
residual.

\label{cor:cpi_dials_main}
Under Corollaries~\ref{cor:epsA_dials} and~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap} and

\eta(\pi_\alpha)

\section{\texorpdfstring{Persistent-$z$}{Persistent-z} on the Augmented MDP}
\label{app:persistent_latent}

\subsection{Direct finite-depth residual certificate}
\label{sec:persistent_augmented_cpi}

The formal statement below applies Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}
on a measurable augmented domain. In particular, it permits a continuous
carried latent $z\in\mathbb R^d$ without reducing it to a countable reachable
set.

Fix $n$, $(\theta,\psi)$, the policy parameters, and all environment kernels. Let $h\in\{1,2,\ldots,H\}$ be the number of edits remaining before the current action. Define
\begin{equation}
F_n(x,y,z):=T_{x,y}^{\circ n}(z)=\widetilde f_\theta^{\circ n}(z,y,x),
\qquad \bar s=(x,y,z,h),
\label{eq:persistent_hn}
\end{equation}
For a nonabsorbing state, first compute $F_n$ and sample an action. If the environment transition terminates for any reason (including a solution or terminal STOP), or if $h=1$, the successor is the shared atom $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$; budget exhaustion is terminal. Otherwise the clock-complete successor is
\[
\bar s'=\bigl(x,\mathrm{edit}(y,a;x),F_n(x,y,z),h-1\bigr).
\]
Thus the state stores the pre-unroll carried latent $z$, the policy uses the post-unroll latent $F_n(x,y,z)$, and a nonterminal successor carries that post-unroll latent.
The current and candidate action kernels share this same frozen $F_n$ and hence the same augmented MDP:
\[
\bar\pi(a\mid\bar s)=\pi_\phi(a\mid y,F_n(x,y,z),x),
\qquad
\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(a\mid\bar s)=\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(a\mid y,F_n(x,y,z),x).
\]
Changing the recurrent map between these two policies would change the transition kernel and is outside ordinary CPI.
\begin{remark}[Why the recurrent map is shared]
\label{rem:shared_map_impossibility}
A small Euclidean discrepancy between deterministic recurrent maps does not
imply a small policy or augmented-kernel discrepancy. For example, take
$F_\varepsilon(z)=-\varepsilon/2$ and
$G_\varepsilon(z)=\varepsilon/2$ and a fixed threshold policy head that
chooses disjoint actions below and above zero. Then
$\sup_z\|F_\varepsilon(z)-G_\varepsilon(z)\|=\varepsilon$, but the action
laws are disjoint and a fixed MDP can have an order-$1/(1-\gamma)$ return
gap. Moreover,
$\operatorname{TV}(\delta_{F(z)},\delta_{G(z)})=1$ whenever
$F(z)\ne G(z)$. A different-map theorem therefore needs explicit reward and
transition-kernel discrepancies and sufficient regularity to propagate them.
Map distance alone is insufficient.
\end{remark}
The initial law is
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho(dx,dy,dz,dh)=\rho(dx,dy,dh)\,\delta_{z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y)}(dz),
\label{eq:persistent_initial_law}
\end{equation}
or, for stochastic initialization,
$\bar\rho(dx,dy,dz,dh)=\rho(dx,dy,dh)\nu_0(dz\mid x,y,h)$ with any recorded
measurable initialization kernel. Ignoring $h$ is an architectural
specialization, not a Bellman requirement.

Assume the initialization, edit/reward kernels, $F_n$, $V_\psi$, and both
policy kernels are measurable, and the $K$-step expected reward and evaluator
are bounded. Let $\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi$ be a measurable
$\bar\pi$-invariant domain with
$\bar\rho(\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi)=1$. Let $\bar{\mathcal C}$ be a measurable
advantage-evaluation domain containing $\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi$ and the shared
absorber, containing the one-step successors from $\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi$ for
$\bar\pi$- and $\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$-almost every action, and invariant
under every subsequent $\bar\pi$ step. In particular,
$\bar\rho(\bar{\mathcal C})=1$. Write
$\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ:=\bar{\mathcal C}\setminus\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$
for its nonabsorbing part. Keep this augmented MDP, including its deployed
depth $n$, fixed. For any finite comparison depth $q\ge0$, define the
endpoint function
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_q(\bar s):=
V_\psi(\widetilde f_\theta^{\circ q}(z,y,x),x,y),
\qquad
\widetilde U_q(s_{\mathrm{abs}}):=-C_{\max}.
\label{eq:persistent_un_value}
\end{equation}
Changing $q$ here changes only a comparison function on the already-fixed
state space. It does not change $F_n$, either policy kernel, or the augmented
transition kernel. Without this restriction, two endpoint depths would
belong to different MDPs and no finite-reference comparison would follow.
At $q=n$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_un_value} is the deployed evaluator.
Let $\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K$ denote the $K$-step Bellman operator of this frozen augmented MDP. Its direct residual is
\begin{equation}
\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}
:=
\|\widetilde U_n-\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_n\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:persistent_direct_residual}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta(\bar\pi')$ denotes the discounted return of any augmented policy $\bar\pi'$ from $\bar\rho$, and $d_{\bar\pi}$ is the discounted occupancy of the current policy. For an augmented advantage estimator $\widehat A$, define
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\bar\pi}(\bar\pi')
&:=\eta(\bar\pi)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{\bar s\sim d_{\bar\pi},\,a\sim\bar\pi'(\cdot\mid\bar s)}[\widehat A(\bar s,a)].
\label{eq:persistent_surrogate}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[Direct persistent-$z$ certificate on the augmented MDP]
\label{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented}
Let $\bar{\mathcal C}$ be the preceding measurable augmented domain and
equip $B_b(\bar{\mathcal C})$ with its
ordinary pointwise sup norm. Under the preceding frozen-MDP, measurability,
bounded-$K$-step-reward, common-boundary, and invariance assumptions, take
$\widetilde U_n\in B_b(\bar{\mathcal C})$. Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}
gives a unique $V^{\bar\pi}\in B_b(\bar{\mathcal C})$ and
\begin{align}
\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le \frac{\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_augmented_value}
\end{align}
If $\widehat A_{\widetilde U_n}$ uses the exact transition expectation and exact baseline and is therefore centered pointwise under $\bar\pi$ on the full augmented state, then Assumption~\ref{ass:Ahat_error_bound} holds for every $\bar s\in\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi$ and for $\bar\pi$- and $\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$-almost every action, with the valid choice
\begin{align}
\bar\varepsilon_A
&:=\frac{2\gamma\,\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_augmented_adv}
\end{align}
If, additionally, the analyzed policy is the pointwise exact mixture
$\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}}=(1-\alpha)\bar\pi+\alpha\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$
with no parameter interpolation or distillation, let
$\bar{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{mix}}$ be a measurable augmented domain carrying
$\bar\rho$, containing $\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi$, and invariant under both $\bar\pi$ and
$\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}}$. Define
\begin{align}
\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}
&:=\sup_{\bar s\in\bar{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{mix}}}
\left|\E_{a\sim\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid\bar s)}
A^{\bar\pi}(\bar s,a)\right|,
\label{eq:persistent_cpi_constant}\\
\bar\tau_\infty
&:=\sup_{\bar s\in\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi}
\operatorname{TV}(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid\bar s),
\bar\pi(\cdot\mid\bar s)),
\end{align}
where
$\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi\subseteq
\bar{\mathcal C}\cap\bar{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{mix}}$
is the common full-measure current-policy domain. Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} gives
\begin{align}
\eta(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
&\ge \widehat L_{\bar\pi}(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\bar\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}
\frac{\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}
-\frac{2\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:persistent_augmented_cpi}
\end{align}

For the target-network analogue, let
$\bar r_K^{\bar\pi}$ be the $K$-step expected reward of this same fixed
augmented MDP. Suppose the conditional target trajectory follows
$\bar\pi$, and take
$\widetilde U_q,\bar V\in B_b(\bar{\mathcal C})$ with the common scalar
absorbing boundary. Define
\begin{align}
\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
&:=
\left\|\widetilde U_q-
\left(\bar r_K^{\bar\pi}
+\gamma^K P_{\bar\pi}^K\bar V\right)
\right\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}},
\label{eq:persistent_target_population_residual}\\
\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
&:=\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
+\gamma^K
\|P_{\bar\pi}^K(\bar V-\widetilde U_q)\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}},
\label{eq:persistent_R_targ_propagated}\\
\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{\sup}
&:=\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{targ},q}
+\gamma^K
\|\bar V-\widetilde U_q\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:persistent_R_targ_sup}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
\|\widetilde U_q-
\bar{\mathcal T}_K^{\bar\pi}\widetilde U_q\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le \bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
\le \bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{\sup},
\label{eq:persistent_target_residual_bridge}\\
\|\widetilde U_q-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le
\frac{\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_target_value_certificate}
\end{align}
For fixed-MDP endpoint depths $0\le n<m$,
\begin{equation}
\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\|\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_m\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\frac{\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},m}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_target_finite_reference}
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat A_{\widetilde U_q}$ be the one-step estimator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app} on the augmented MDP, with its transition
expectation and current-policy baseline evaluated exactly conditional on the
full augmented state. Define
\begin{equation}
\bar\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
:=\sup_{\bar s\in\bar{\mathcal C}_\pi}
\left|
\E_{a\sim\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid\bar s)}
\left[
\widehat A_{\widetilde U_q}(\bar s,a)-A^{\bar\pi}(\bar s,a)
\right]
\right|.
\label{eq:persistent_target_candidate_bias_def}
\end{equation}
Exact baseline evaluation centers this estimator conditional on the full
augmented state, and the candidate bias satisfies
\begin{equation}
\bar\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
\le
2\gamma\bar\tau_\infty
\frac{\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:persistent_target_candidate_bias}
\end{equation}
The target-network CPI and finite-reference CPI forms are therefore
\begin{align}
\eta(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
&\ge \widehat L_{\bar\pi}(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\bar\tau_\infty}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma^K)}\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
-\frac{2\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)},
\label{eq:persistent_target_cpi}\\
\eta(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
&\ge \widehat L_{\bar\pi}(\bar\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\bar\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}
\left(
\|\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_m\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\frac{\bar R_{\mathrm{targ},m}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}
\right)
-\frac{2\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:persistent_target_finite_reference_cpi}
\end{align}
The last display uses the same exact value-derived construction at
$\widetilde U_n$. Every target formula
keeps the propagated lag before the ordinary sup-lag fallback.
\end{corollary}

This direct result is Bellman contraction on the actual finite-depth augmented evaluator. It requires no recurrent latent fixed point, recurrent contraction, Lipschitz head, projection, or slow-drift assumption; it still uses the value-function fixed point $V^{\bar\pi}$. The augmented residual differs from the episodic residual on $(x,y,h)$ because both the policy and transition can depend on the carried latent. A learned instantiation would additionally need to establish the uniform augmented residual, exact augmented-state centering, and pointwise exact-mixture deployment. Empirical regression loss, a finite-batch maximum, and population $L^2$ error establish neither the target population sup norm nor either required target-lag sup norm.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the direct augmented-state certificate]
\label{rem:persistent_augmented_scope}
Corollary~\ref{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented} is a result on $\bar s=(x,y,z,h)$, not on the plan marginal $(x,y)$. Its exact-centering condition averages actions conditional on the full $\bar s$, and its mixture identity must hold pointwise on the augmented mixture-occupancy domain $\bar{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{mix}}$. Finite-batch maxima are diagnostics, not uniform certificates.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-path carried-latent comparison]
\label{lem:persistent_finite_path}
Let $z_{t+1}=F_t(z_t)$ for $t=0,\ldots,T-1$, and let
$\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_T$ be an arbitrary reference path. Suppose along these
paths that, for every $t=0,\ldots,T-1$,
\[
\|F_t(z_t)-F_t(\xi_t)\|
\le
\kappa_t\|z_t-\xi_t\|,
\qquad
d_t:=\|F_t(\xi_t)-\xi_{t+1}\|.
\]
Then, with empty products equal to one,
\begin{equation}
\|z_t-\xi_t\|
\le
\|z_0-\xi_0\|\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\kappa_i
+
\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}d_j\prod_{i=j+1}^{t-1}\kappa_i.
\label{eq:persistent_finite_path}
\end{equation}
If a head evaluated directly at $z_t$ is $L_{V,t}$-Lipschitz, its error is
at most $L_{V,t}$ times the right-hand side. In the architecture used here,
the state stores the pre-unroll $z_t$ and the head reads $F_t(z_t)$; relative
to $F_t(\xi_t)$ its error is therefore at most
$L_{V,t}\kappa_t\|z_t-\xi_t\|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing $e_t=\|z_t-\xi_t\|$, the triangle inequality gives
$e_{t+1}\le\kappa_t e_t+d_t$. Induction gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_finite_path}; head Lipschitzness gives the final
claims. This is a pathwise statement. It becomes a uniform sup-norm
certificate only when the initial error, $\kappa_t$, $d_t$, and $L_{V,t}$
are bounded uniformly over every declared nonabsorbing start and trajectory.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optional slow-drift comparison to a fixed-point reference}
\label{sec:twotimescale_persistent}

Slow drift is needed only for the stronger architectural comparison between
the actual transient evaluator and the memoryless reference
$\widetilde U_*(x,y,z,h):=V_\psi(z^*(x,y),x,y)$, with
$\widetilde U_*(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=-C_{\max}$. For this subsection, invoke
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} with the declared domain
$\mathcal D^\circ=\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ$. Thus $z^*(x,y)$ denotes the
unique fixed point only for input pairs represented by this augmented
closure; no claim is made about other pairs. Assume
$(\bar s,u)\mapsto\widetilde f_\theta(u,y(\bar s),x(\bar s))$ is jointly
measurable on
$\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ\times\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ and impose a
measurable iteration initialization
$z^{(0)}:\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ\to\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:banach} then makes the
fixed-point map measurable. Invoke
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded_value_head} with
$\mathcal D^\circ=\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ$ and
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{bd}}=\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$; with the separate
finite absorbing boundary, $\widetilde U_*$ is bounded and measurable on the
augmented closure. Assume the tracking conditions below hold uniformly
over nonabsorbing trajectory segments formed by one action drawn under either
policy followed by $\bar\pi$ dynamics, and assume every carried latent on
those segments lies in $\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$. These segments include
$\bar\rho$-almost every initial augmented state and every starting state used
to generate a one-deviation trajectory in the augmented closure. In the
stochastic-initialization case, require
$\nu_0(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\mid x,y,h)=1$ for $\rho$-almost every
$(x,y,h)$. Let $e:\YY\to\mathcal E_y$ be a fixed plan embedding and write
$\|y-y'\|_{\mathcal E}:=\|e(y)-e(y')\|_{\mathcal E_y}$.

\begin{assumption}[Slow drift of fixed points]
\label{ass:twotimescale}
Uniformly over the nonabsorbing augmented trajectory segments declared above,
there exist $L_{z^*}\ge0$, $\Delta y_{\max}\ge0$, and $E_0<\infty$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every consecutive source/successor pair with common instance $x$
and represented plans $y_t,y_{t+1}$,
$\|z^*(x,y_t)-z^*(x,y_{t+1})\|
\le L_{z^*}\|y_t-y_{t+1}\|_{\mathcal E}$;
\item every admissible edit satisfies $\|y_{t+1}-y_t\|_{\mathcal E}\le\Delta y_{\max}$; and
\item for $\bar\rho$-almost every $(x,y_0,z_0,h_0)$, and uniformly for every augmented state treated as time zero when constructing a one-deviation trajectory in the policy-pair closure, the carried latent satisfies $\|z_0-z^*(x,y_0)\|\le E_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[A conditional sufficient bound for $L_{z^*}$]
\label{rem:Lzstar_implicit}
If $y$ has a specified differentiable continuous representation, $f_\theta$ is $C^1$ near an interior fixed point, and $\|\partial_z f_\theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le L_z<1$, the implicit-function calculation gives the local derivative bound
\begin{equation}
\|D_y z^*(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac{\|D_y f_\theta(z^*(x,y),y,x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{1-L_z}.
\label{eq:Lzstar_bound}
\end{equation}
This pointwise statement does not by itself supply the single uniform
$L_{z^*}$ required by Assumption~\ref{ass:twotimescale}. For every represented
consecutive plan pair, that conclusion additionally needs a $C^1$ fixed-point
branch along a connecting path and one derivative bound uniform over all such
paths. For discrete Sudoku plans, the manuscript provides and verifies no such
extension.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Discrete-plan scope]
\label{rem:reasoning_manifold}
For a discrete domain, Assumption~\ref{ass:twotimescale} is simply an additional regularity assumption in the stated embedding. Successful training does not by itself establish it, and finite-batch diagnostics do not establish the uniform assumption.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the slow-drift assumption]
\label{rem:slowdrift_scope}
Only the augmented closure for the frozen policy pair is relevant; no global smoothness claim outside that closure is needed.
\end{remark}

For this optional subsection fix $n\ge 1$. At nonabsorbing times define
$e_t=\|z_t-z^*(x,y_t)\|$ and $\kappa_n=L_z^n<1$.
\begin{lemma}[Drift bound for persistent-$z$]
\label{lem:drift_bound}
Under the $\mathcal D^\circ=\bar{\mathcal C}^\circ$ invocation of
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} and Assumption~\ref{ass:twotimescale}, every
transition for which both $\bar s_t$ and $\bar s_{t+1}$ are nonabsorbing satisfies
\begin{equation}
e_{t+1}\le\kappa_n e_t+L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max},
\label{eq:tracking_recursion}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\sup_{t:\,\bar s_t\ne s_{\mathrm{abs}}}\|z_t^{(n)}-z^*(x,y_t)\|\le\Cdrift(n)
:=\kappa_n\max\!\left(E_0,\frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}\right)
\le\kappa_n E_0+\frac{\kappa_nL_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}.
\label{eq:Cdrift_def}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:drift_bound} is the uniform specialization of
Lemma~\ref{lem:persistent_finite_path} with
$F_t=T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}$, $\xi_t=z^*(x,y_t)$,
$\kappa_t=L_z^n$, and
$d_t\le L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}$. The additional factor $\kappa_n$ in
$\Cdrift(n)$ appears because the value head reads the post-unroll latent.

\begin{remark}[When the drift bound is informative]
\label{rem:drift_meaningful}
The bound is useful only when its assumed constants are finite and calibrated on the policy-pair closure. Otherwise the direct residual certificate remains mathematically valid but uncalibrated, while the fixed-point comparison is vacuous.
\end{remark}

Define
\begin{equation}
\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},*}
:=\|\widetilde U_*-\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_*\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:persistent_fixed_residual}
\end{equation}
Then Lipschitzness of the head and Lemma~\ref{lem:drift_bound} give
\begin{align}
\|\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_*\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le L_V\Cdrift(n),
\label{eq:persistent_fixed_comparison}\\
\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le L_V\Cdrift(n)+\frac{\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},*}}{1-\gamma^K},
\label{eq:persistent_slow_drift_value}\\
|\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}-\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},*}|
&\le(1+\gamma^K)L_V\Cdrift(n).
\label{eq:persistent_residual_relation}
\end{align}
These are optional comparisons to a memoryless fixed-point reference; none is needed for Corollary~\ref{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented}.

\begin{definition}[$\pi$-invariant Bellman domain]
\label{def:reachable}
A measurable set $\mathcal C_\pi$ is a Bellman/value domain for $\pi$ if
$\rho(\mathcal C_\pi)=1$ whenever the initial law is used and
\[
P(\mathcal C_\pi\mid s,a)=1
\quad\text{for $\pi(da\mid s)$-almost every $a$ and every
$s\in\mathcal C_\pi$.}
\]
Equivalently, the policy-induced kernel leaves $\mathcal C_\pi$ invariant.
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} needs only such a domain and does
not need a candidate policy.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Advantage-evaluation domain]
\label{def:adv_closure}
Fix current and candidate stochastic kernels $\pi$ and
$\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$. A measurable set
$\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ contains a
$\pi$-invariant domain $\mathcal C_\pi$ and the common absorber and satisfies
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $s\in\mathcal C_\pi$,
$P(\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}\mid s,a)=1$
for $\pi(da\mid s)$- and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(da\mid s)$-almost every
action; and
\item for every
$s\in\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$,
$P(\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}\mid s,a)=1$
for $\pi(da\mid s)$-almost every action.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the domain contains current-policy evaluation states, one current or
candidate action, and every subsequent current-policy continuation. We write
$\|f\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ for the ordinary
supremum on this set, including the scalar absorbing boundary.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Mixture-occupancy and deployment domains]
\label{def:mixture_deployment_domains}
For a fixed $\alpha$, a measurable $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is a
mixture-occupancy domain if $\rho(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}})=1$ and it is
invariant under both $\pi$ and
$\pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$. It therefore supports
the state laws at every time under the current policy and under repeated
mixture decisions, not merely a one-deviation path. A deployment domain
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$ satisfies
$\rho(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}})=1$ and is recursively invariant under both
$\pi_\alpha$ and $\widetilde\pi$. The CPI span theorem uses
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$; the deployment theorem uses
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$.

For a certificate simultaneous over more than one mixture weight, a
measurable common policy-pair domain $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\rho(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}})=1,
\qquad
P_\pi(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}\mid s)
=P_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}\mid s)=1
\quad\text{for every }s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}.
\label{eq:pair_domain_invariance}
\end{equation}
By linearity of policy-induced kernels, every exact mixture $\pi_\alpha$
also leaves $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ invariant. Thus
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ is a valid single choice of
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ for every $\alpha\in[0,1]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Recovering candidate invariance from a positive mixture]
\label{rem:positive_mixture_pair_domain}
Let $\alpha_0>0$. If a measurable set $\mathcal E$ is invariant under
$\pi$ and $\pi_{\alpha_0}$, then for every $s\in\mathcal E$,
\[
1=P_{\pi_{\alpha_0}}(\mathcal E\mid s)
=(1-\alpha_0)P_\pi(\mathcal E\mid s)
+\alpha_0P_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}(\mathcal E\mid s),
\]
and $P_\pi(\mathcal E\mid s)=1$ forces
$P_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}(\mathcal E\mid s)=1$. This implication does not
follow at $\alpha_0=0$ without separately assuming candidate invariance.
\end{remark}

In finite or countable spaces these domains can be constructed with the usual
positive-probability reachable sets and action supports. On arbitrary
measurable action spaces we use the policy-almost-every-action clauses above;
no topological action support is presumed.

\paragraph{Why boundedness matters.}
On the countable zero-reward chain $s_i\mapsto s_{i+1}$ with one action and
$\gamma\in(0,1)$, add an unreachable zero-reward absorber with
$U(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=V^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=0$. The unbounded function
$U(s_i)=\gamma^{-i}$ has zero $K$-step residual for every $K\ge1$, while
its sup-norm error from $V^\pi=0$ is infinite. This is why the residual
theorem works on bounded functions, not arbitrary pointwise solutions.

\begin{remark}[Why the primary extension uses $B_b$]
\label{rem:general_kernel_extension}
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} works directly on
$B_b(\mathcal C)$ and needs no dominating measure. An alternative formulation
on equivalence classes in a common $L^\infty(\nu)$ would additionally need
initial and occupancy domination by $\nu$, $\nu$-nonsingularity of the
relevant kernels, representative-independent Bellman integration, and the
corresponding joint measurability conditions. Occupancy comparisons for
general measures use total variation; vector $\ell_1$ notation is reserved
for finite or countable spaces.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A weighted-\texorpdfstring{$L_p$}{Lp} fixed-policy restatement}
\label{app:weighted_lp}

A weighted norm can replace the population supremum when a reference
measure covers the fixed policy's discounted occupancy. The following result
states the corresponding change-of-measure cost. For
a probability measure $\nu$ and $1\le p<\infty$, write
\(
\|f\|_{p,\nu}:=(\int_{\mathcal C}|f|^p\,d\nu)^{1/p}.
\)

\begin{proposition}[Weighted-$L_p$ finite-reference bound]
\label{prop:weighted_finite_reference}
Under the measurable fixed-policy hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}, let $\rho$ and $\mu$ be probability
measures on $\mathcal C$, let $1\le p<\infty$, and take bounded measurable
endpoints $U_n,U_m$. Set
\begin{equation}
\beta:=\gamma^K,
\qquad
P:=P_\pi^K,
\qquad
r_m:=U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m,
\label{eq:weighted_objects}
\end{equation}
and define the discounted occupancy of the $K$-step skeleton by
\begin{equation}
d_\rho^{\pi,K}
:=(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^t\rho P^t.
\label{eq:k_skeleton_occupancy}
\end{equation}
This is a probability measure on $\mathcal C$. Suppose
$d_\rho^{\pi,K}\ll\mu$ and
\begin{equation}
C_{\rho,\mu}^{\pi,K}
:=
\left\|
\frac{d d_\rho^{\pi,K}}{d\mu}
\right\|_{\infty,\mu}
<\infty,
\label{eq:fixed_kernel_occupancy_ratio}
\end{equation}
where the norm is the $\mu$-essential supremum. Then
\begin{equation}
\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{p,\rho}
\le
\|U_n-U_m\|_{p,\rho}
+
\frac{(C_{\rho,\mu}^{\pi,K})^{1/p}}{1-\gamma^K}
\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_{p,\mu}.
\label{eq:weighted_finite_reference}
\end{equation}
If also $\rho\ll\mu$ and
\begin{equation}
C_{\rho,\mu}^{(0)}
:=
\left\|\frac{d\rho}{d\mu}\right\|_{\infty,\mu}
<\infty,
\label{eq:initial_density_ratio}
\end{equation}
then the endpoint discrepancy may instead be measured under $\mu$:
\begin{equation}
\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{p,\rho}
\le
(C_{\rho,\mu}^{(0)})^{1/p}\|U_n-U_m\|_{p,\mu}
+
\frac{(C_{\rho,\mu}^{\pi,K})^{1/p}}{1-\gamma^K}
\|r_m\|_{p,\mu}.
\label{eq:weighted_finite_reference_common_measure}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_m:=U_m-V^\pi$. Subtracting the fixed-point identity
$V^\pi=\mathcal T_K^\pi V^\pi$ from the definition of $r_m$ gives
\begin{equation}
e_m=r_m+\beta Pe_m.
\label{eq:weighted_resolvent_identity}
\end{equation}
Because $e_m$ and $r_m$ are bounded and $\beta<1$, iteration of this identity
and uniform decay of the remainder yield
\begin{equation}
e_m=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^tP^tr_m,
\qquad
|e_m|\le\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^tP^t|r_m|.
\label{eq:weighted_resolvent_series}
\end{equation}
Define the Markov kernel
\(
Q:=(1-\beta)\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tP^t.
\)
Then $\rho Q=d_\rho^{\pi,K}$ and
$|e_m|\le Q|r_m|/(1-\beta)$. Jensen's inequality for the Markov kernel $Q$
gives
\begin{align}
\|e_m\|_{p,\rho}^p
&\le
\frac{1}{(1-\beta)^p}
\int_{\mathcal C}Q|r_m|^p\,d\rho
\notag\\
&=
\frac{1}{(1-\beta)^p}
\int_{\mathcal C}|r_m|^p\,d d_\rho^{\pi,K}
\notag\\
&\le
\frac{C_{\rho,\mu}^{\pi,K}}{(1-\beta)^p}
\|r_m\|_{p,\mu}^p.
\label{eq:weighted_residual_step}
\end{align}
Taking $p$th roots and applying the triangle inequality proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_finite_reference}. Under
Eq.~\eqref{eq:initial_density_ratio}, the same change-of-measure calculation
gives
$\|U_n-U_m\|_{p,\rho}\le
(C_{\rho,\mu}^{(0)})^{1/p}\|U_n-U_m\|_{p,\mu}$,
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_finite_reference_common_measure}.
\end{proof}

The contraction and resolvent in Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted_finite_reference}
are generated by the Bellman operator $\mathcal T_K^\pi$ and the fixed
$K$-step kernel $P_\pi^K$. The learned recurrence is not a replacement Bellman
operator. It determines the endpoint functions and, in persistent mode, is
part of the frozen augmented transition kernel. The coefficient in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_kernel_occupancy_ratio} concerns this one fixed kernel; it
does not include a supremum over changing policy sequences, a function-class
inherent Bellman error, or a sampling term. Thus the proposition is an
a posteriori fixed-snapshot analogue, not the weighted-norm API/FVI results of
Munos~\citep{Munos2003API} or Munos and
Szepesv\'ari~\citep{MunosSzepesvari2008}.

Domination can be especially restrictive for persistent deterministic
latents. Augmented occupancies may lie on lower-dimensional graphs, so a
generic product reference measure with Lebesgue measure on the latent can

distribution may have poor or singular coverage. Finite reachable-set or
finite-population diagnostics do not establish the continuous-state
population coefficient.

\section{Exact Finite-MDP Derivation}
\label{app:finite_mdp_derivation}

This section derives Table~\ref{tab:finite_mdp_summary}. It uses exact
rational arithmetic and no data, learned parameter, or random sample. The
state and action definitions are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccl@{}}
\toprule
State & Action & $(r,S')$ \\
\midrule
$s_0$ & $a$ & $(0,s_0)$ \\
$s_0$ & $b$ & $(1,s_1)$ \\
$s_1$ & $a$ & $(0,s_0)$ \\
$s_1$ & $b$ & $(0,s_1)$ \\
$s_{\mathrm{abs}}$ & $a$ or $b$ & $(0,s_{\mathrm{abs}})$ \\

The current policy is $\pi(a\mid s)=1$, the candidate is
$\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(b\mid s)=1$, and $\rho=\delta_{s_0}$. These definitions
also apply at the absorber, where both actions induce the same zero-reward
self-loop. Thus
\[
\mathcal C_\pi=\{s_0,s_{\mathrm{abs}}\},\qquad
\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}
=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}=\mathcal S.
\]
The first set is $\pi$-invariant and carries $\rho$. One candidate deviation
from $s_0$ reaches $s_1$, after which $\pi$ returns to $s_0$. Repeated mixture
decisions remain in $\mathcal S$, so this is the complete relevant closure.

\paragraph{Finite-reference value calculation.}
Set $\gamma=1/2$, $K=1$, $n=1$, and $m=2$. On the scalar latent space, use
the common initialization $z^{(0)}(s)=0$, head $V_\psi(z,s)=z$, and
\[
T_s(z)=u_s-\frac12z,\qquad
(u_{s_0},u_{s_1},u_{s_{\mathrm{abs}}})
=\left(\frac14,-\frac14,0\right).
\]
The absorber therefore has the common depth-independent boundary zero. The
finite endpoints are
\[
U_1=\left(\frac14,-\frac14,0\right),\qquad
U_2=\left(\frac18,-\frac18,0\right).
\]
Under $\pi$, all nonabsorbing rewards are zero and both nonabsorbing states
move to $s_0$, so $V^\pi=(0,0,0)$. Moreover,
\[
\mathcal T_1^\pi U_2
=\left(\frac1{16},\frac1{16},0\right),\qquad
U_2-\mathcal T_1^\pi U_2
=\left(\frac1{16},-\frac3{16},0\right).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\|U_1-U_2\|_\infty&=\frac18,
&
\|U_2-\mathcal T_1^\pi U_2\|_\infty&=\frac3{16},\\
\|U_1-V^\pi\|_\infty
&\le \frac18+\frac{3/16}{1-1/2}=\frac12,
&
\|U_1-V^\pi\|_\infty&=\frac14.
\end{align*}
The certificate gives the finite error radius $1/2$ without a recurrent
fixed-point argument. It is not tight: the actual error is smaller by a factor
of two.

\paragraph{Exact mixture and occupancies.}
Take $\alpha=1/2$. At every state,
$\pi_{1/2}(a\mid s)=\pi_{1/2}(b\mid s)=1/2$. On the two nonabsorbing states,
\[
P_{\pi_{1/2}}
=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
r_{\pi_{1/2}}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The absorber is a separate zero-reward self-loop. Solving
$d=(1-\gamma)\rho+\gamma dP$ gives
\[
d_\pi=(1,0,0),\qquad
d_{\pi_{1/2}}=\left(\frac34,\frac14,0\right).
\]
Solving the mixture Bellman equations gives
$V^{\pi_{1/2}}=(3/4,1/4,0)$ and hence
$\eta(\pi_{1/2})=3/4$.

\paragraph{Advantage, centering, and CPI lower bound.}
Because $V^\pi=0$, the candidate expected advantage is
\[
g=(1,0,0),\qquad \Delta_g=1.
\]
For the value-derived one-step estimator based on $U_1$, the exact action
values on $(s_0,s_1)$ are
\[
\widehat Q_{U_1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1/8&7/8\\1/8&-1/8\end{pmatrix},
\]
with columns $(a,b)$. Exact centering under the deterministic current policy
subtracts the first column. Including the absorber gives
\[
\widehat A(a)=(0,0,0),\qquad
\widehat A(b)=\left(\frac34,-\frac14,0\right).
\]
Thus the centering defect and candidate defect from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:signed_defects} are
\[
c=(0,0,0),\qquad
b=\left(-\frac14,-\frac14,0\right),\qquad
\Xi_{1/2}=-\frac18.
\]
The estimated surrogate is
\[
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_{1/2})
=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\pi,a\sim\pi_{1/2}}\widehat A(s,a)
=2\left(\frac12\cdot\frac34\right)=\frac34.
\]
The exact span penalty is
\[
\frac{\gamma\alpha^2\Delta_g}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}
=\frac13.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:main_theorem_occupancy} therefore yields
\[
\eta(\pi_{1/2})
\ge \frac34-\frac{-1/8}{1/2}-\frac13
=\frac23,
\]
while the exact return is $3/4$. In contrast, the absolute uniform
specialization uses $\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}=1/4$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}=1$, producing
\[
\frac34-\frac14-\frac23=-\frac16.
\]
This valid but vacuous bound shows why the signed defect and the advantage
span matter. Run \texttt{python3 UPI\_TRM\_ICLR/verify\_finite\_mdp.py} from
the repository root to reproduce the calculation.

\section{Shaping Convention and Absorbing-State Normalization}
\label{app:shaping_convention}

We use potential-based shaping with potential $\Phi(s)=c(x,y)$ for episodic $s=(x,y,h)$ and persistent $\bar s=(x,y,z,h)$ (the potential ignores the clock and latent), and $\Phi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=C_{\max}$ on their shared absorbing atom. We define
\begin{equation}
r(s,a,s')
=
r_0(s,a,s')
+
\gamma\,\Phi(s')-\Phi(s),
\end{equation}
applying shaping on \emph{all} transitions, including the absorbing self-loop.

For the absorbing self-loop, taking $r_0(s_{\mathrm{abs}},a,s_{\mathrm{abs}})=0$ gives
\[
r(s_{\mathrm{abs}},a,s_{\mathrm{abs}})
=
\gamma \Phi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})-\Phi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})
=
(\gamma-1)C_{\max}.
\]
Thus every policy satisfies the fixed shaped boundary value
\[
V^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})
=
\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t (\gamma-1)C_{\max}
=
-C_{\max}.
\]

If one prefers the standard normalization $V(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=0$, use the shifted potential
$\tilde{\Phi}(s):=\Phi(s)-C_{\max}$.
This adds the constant reward $(1-\gamma)C_{\max}$ to every transition and shifts all returns by $+C_{\max}$ independently of the policy.
Accordingly, performance differences and optimal policies are unchanged.

\section{Finite-Horizon and Deployment-Aware Bounds}
\label{app:finite_horizon_extensions}

The remaining edit budget permits a stagewise analysis. Residual errors are
propagated only until the common terminal boundary, replacing infinite
geometric amplification by a finite sum. The CPI and deployment results
below likewise count only decisions within that horizon.

This section uses the finite edit clock directly. Fix $0\le\gamma<1$, an
integer horizon $H\ge0$, and one shared absorbing atom with boundary value
\[
b:=V_h^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=-C_{\max}
\quad\text{for every }h,
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{abs}}=(1-\gamma)b.
\]
Thus $b=r_{\mathrm{abs}}+\gamma b$. Every reward interval below includes
$r_{\mathrm{abs}}$.

For each $h\in\{0,\ldots,H\}$, let $\mathcal C_h$ be a measurable stage
domain containing $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$, with
\[
\mathcal C_h^\circ:=\mathcal C_h\setminus\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_0=\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}.
\]
Value norms use $\|f\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}:=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_h}|f(s)|$; latent paths are defined only on
$\mathcal C_h^\circ$.

Let $\mathsf Q_h(dr,ds'\mid s,a)$ be the measurable controlled
reward-transition kernel from stage $h$ to stage $h-1$, let $P_h$ be its
state marginal, and let $\pi_h$ be the current-policy stochastic kernel.
Assume its induced kernel satisfies
$P_h^\pi(\mathcal C_{h-1}\mid s)=1$ for every $s\in\mathcal C_h$. If an
initial law is used, assume $\rho(\mathcal C_H)=1$. Assume the rewards and
conditional expected rewards below are bounded and measurable. For
$1\le\ell\le h$ and $W\in B_b(\mathcal C_{h-\ell})$ with
$W(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b$, define
\begin{align}
(\mathcal T_{h:\ell}^{\pi}W)(s)
&:=\E\!\left[
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\gamma^i r_i
+\gamma^\ell W(S_\ell)
\,\middle|\,
S_0=s,\ A_i\sim\pi_{h-i}(\cdot\mid S_i)
\right],
\label{eq:fh_stage_operator}
\end{align}
where an absorbed trajectory remains at the shared atom. Padding after early
termination with absorbing self-loops and applying $b$ at the end of the
block account for the absorbing tail exactly once. These are
current-policy Bellman/value domains; the repeated-mixture domains used for
CPI are defined separately below.

\begin{theorem}[Measurable finite-horizon residual and finite-reference bound]
\label{thm:finite_horizon_reference}
Fix $K\ge1$. Let $U_{h,q}\in B_b(\mathcal C_h)$ for every
$h\in\{0,\ldots,H\}$ and every finite depth $q\ge0$, and impose
\begin{equation}
U_{h,q}(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b,
\qquad
U_{0,q}\equiv b
\quad\text{for every finite }q\ge0.
\label{eq:fh_common_boundary}
\end{equation}
The true stage value satisfies $V_0^\pi\equiv b$ and
$V_h^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b$. For $h\ge1$, set
\begin{align}
\ell_h&:=\min\{K,h\},
&J_h&:=\left\lceil\frac{h}{K}\right\rceil,
\notag\\
\epsilon_{h,q}
&:=\left\|U_{h,q}
-\mathcal T_{h:\ell_h}^{\pi}U_{h-\ell_h,q}\right\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h},
&D_{h;n,m}&:=\|U_{h,n}-U_{h,m}\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h},
\label{eq:fh_residual_def}
\end{align}
Then, for finite depths $0\le n<m$,
\begin{align}
\|U_{h,n}-V_h^\pi\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}
&\le D_{h;n,m}
+\sum_{j=0}^{J_h-1}\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-jK,m}.
\label{eq:fh_finite_reference}
\end{align}
Consequently, with
$\epsilon_m^{(h)}:=\max_{1\le r\le h}\epsilon_{r,m}$,
\begin{align}
\|U_{h,n}-V_h^\pi\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}
&\le D_{h;n,m}
+\frac{1-\gamma^{KJ_h}}{1-\gamma^K}\epsilon_m^{(h)}.
\label{eq:fh_finite_reference_uniform}
\end{align}
Taking the same finite depth as the reference gives
\begin{align}
\|U_{h,q}-V_h^\pi\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}
&\le\sum_{j=0}^{J_h-1}\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-jK,q}.
\label{eq:fh_finite_depth_residual}
\end{align}

For the recurrent specialization, fix one measurable frozen recurrent map
$F$, a measurable initialization on each $\mathcal C_h^\circ$, a latent
norm, and a bounded measurable scalar head $V_\psi$. Define only on
$\mathcal C_h^\circ$
\[
z_h^{(q+1)}(s)=F(z_h^{(q)}(s),s),
\qquad
U_{h,q}(s)=V_\psi(z_h^{(q)}(s),x(s),y(s)).
\]
For persistent state, $z_h^{(0)}(x,y,z,h)=z$ is the carried pre-unroll
latent. An initialization that ignores $h$ is an architectural
specialization, not a premise of the residual argument. At the absorber use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_common_boundary}; no absorbing latent is defined. If the
head is uniformly $L_V$-Lipschitz in the latent argument, then
\begin{align}
D_{h;n,m}
&\le L_V\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_h^\circ}
\|z_h^{(n)}(s)-z_h^{(m)}(s)\|
\label{eq:fh_endpoint_bound}\\
&\le L_V\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_h^\circ}
\sum_{q=n}^{m-1}
\|z_h^{(q+1)}(s)-z_h^{(q)}(s)\|
\label{eq:fh_path_bound_exact}\\
&\le L_V\sum_{q=n}^{m-1}
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_h^\circ}
\|z_h^{(q+1)}(s)-z_h^{(q)}(s)\|.
\label{eq:fh_path_bound}
\end{align}
For $h=0$, set $J_0=0$, $D_{0;n,m}=0$, and interpret every residual and path
sum as empty. If $\mathcal C_h^\circ$ is empty, the nonnegative latent
suprema in the recurrent clause are defined to be zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed depth $q$, write
$E_{h,q}:=\|U_{h,q}-V_h^\pi\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}$ and $E_{0,q}=0$.
The stage Bellman recursion and the Markov-kernel expectation inequality give
\begin{align*}
E_{h,q}
&\le \epsilon_{h,q}
+\left\|\mathcal T_{h:\ell_h}^\pi U_{h-\ell_h,q}
-\mathcal T_{h:\ell_h}^\pi V_{h-\ell_h}^\pi
\right\|_{\infty,\mathcal C_h}\\
&\le \epsilon_{h,q}+\gamma^{\ell_h}E_{h-\ell_h,q}.
\end{align*}
On trajectories absorbed inside the block, the terminal-function difference
is zero because both functions equal $b$. Unrolling visits stages
$h,h-K,\ldots,h-(J_h-1)K$; the last partial block ends at $E_{0,q}=0$.
This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_finite_depth_residual}. Adding and subtracting
$U_{h,m}$ proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_finite_reference}; the uniform residual
bound gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_finite_reference_uniform}. Finally, head
Lipschitzness gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_endpoint_bound}; the triangle inequality
for the pointwise telescoping path gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_path_bound_exact},
and sup--sum interchange gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_path_bound}.
\end{proof}

In a persistent-state application, the state is $(x,y,z,h)$. Whenever the
current and candidate policies are analyzed in one augmented MDP, both use
the same frozen recurrent map for action selection and for the carried
post-unroll latent on each nonterminal transition.

For finite-horizon policy improvement, define
\[
\eta_H(\pi):=\E\!\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t r_t+\gamma^H b
\right],
\]
where early terminal trajectories are padded by the absorbing self-loop.
Let $d_t^\pi$ be the state law before decision $t$, including absorbed mass.
For each $h$, let $\mathcal M_h$ be a measurable stagewise
mixture-occupancy domain containing $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$, with
$\mathcal M_0=\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$ and $\rho(\mathcal M_H)=1$. Require the
controlled successor kernel to satisfy
\[
P_h(\mathcal M_{h-1}\mid s,a)=1
\]
for every $s\in\mathcal M_h$ and for $\pi_h(da\mid s)$- and
$\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}(da\mid s)$-almost every action. This recursive
invariance implies
$d_{H-h}^{\pi}(\mathcal M_h)=
d_{H-h}^{\pi_\alpha}(\mathcal M_h)=1$ and covers a candidate first step
followed by every current-policy continuation. No topological action support
is presumed. Define every stage advantage to be zero at the absorbing atom.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon CPI with stagewise spans]
\label{thm:finite_horizon_cpi}
Fix bounded measurable stage rewards and the common terminal boundary $b$.
For every $h\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$, fix a current-policy kernel $\pi_h$, a
candidate kernel $\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}$, and the exact pointwise
probability-space mixture
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\alpha,h}(\cdot\mid s)
=(1-\alpha)\pi_h(\cdot\mid s)
+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}(\cdot\mid s),
\qquad\alpha\in[0,1].
\label{eq:fh_exact_mixture}
\end{equation}
All policies act in the same fixed time-indexed MDP. Assume the relevant
values, advantages, and estimator functions are bounded and measurable on
$\mathcal M_h$. Define
\begin{align}
g_h(s)
&:=\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}(\cdot\mid s)}[A_h^\pi(s,a)],
&\Delta_h
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal M_h}g_h(s)
-\inf_{s\in\mathcal M_h}g_h(s),
\label{eq:fh_cpi_span}
\end{align}
with $g_h(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=0$. Let $\widehat A_h$ be exactly centered:
\begin{align}
\E_{a\sim\pi_h(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A_h(s,a)]&=0
\quad\text{for every }s\in\mathcal M_h,
\label{eq:fh_centering}
\end{align}
and define the signed candidate defect
\begin{align}
\beta_h(s)
&:=
\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}(\cdot\mid s)}
[\widehat A_h(s,a)-A_h^\pi(s,a)].
\label{eq:fh_candidate_defect}
\end{align}
Set
\begin{equation}
\Xi_{H,\alpha}:=
\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\E_{s\sim d_t^\pi}[\beta_{H-t}(s)].
\label{eq:fh_signed_defect}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{H,\pi}(\pi_\alpha)
&:=\eta_H(\pi)+\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\E_{\substack{s\sim d_t^\pi,\\
a\sim\pi_{\alpha,H-t}(\cdot\mid s)}}
[\widehat A_{H-t}(s,a)].
\label{eq:fh_estimated_surrogate}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
\eta_H(\pi_\alpha)
\ge{}&\widehat L_{H,\pi}(\pi_\alpha)
-\Xi_{H,\alpha}
\notag\\
&-\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\Delta_{H-t}[1-(1-\alpha)^t].
\label{eq:fh_cpi_exact_coupling}
\end{align}
Because $\Xi_{H,\alpha}$ is signed, any established upper bound
$B_{H,\alpha}\ge\Xi_{H,\alpha}$ may replace it. One valid choice is
\begin{align}
B_{H,\alpha}
&:=\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\E_{s\sim d_t^\pi}|\beta_{H-t}(s)|.
\label{eq:fh_defect_upper_bound}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The time-indexed performance-difference identity is
\begin{align}
\eta_H(\pi_\alpha)-\eta_H(\pi)
&=\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\E_{s\sim d_t^{\pi_\alpha}}[g_{H-t}(s)].
\label{eq:fh_performance_difference}
\end{align}
The common terminal term cancels. Represent each mixture decision by an
independent Bernoulli selector. Before decision $t$, the first $t$ selectors
have all chosen the current-policy component with probability
$(1-\alpha)^t$. Conditional on that event the state law is $d_t^\pi$, so for
some probability law $\nu_t$,
\[
d_t^{\pi_\alpha}=(1-\alpha)^t d_t^\pi
+[1-(1-\alpha)^t]\nu_t.
\]
Therefore
\begin{align}
\operatorname{TV}(d_t^{\pi_\alpha},d_t^\pi)
&\le1-(1-\alpha)^t.
\label{eq:fh_occupancy_coupling}
\end{align}
The exponent is $t$, not $t+1$, because $d_t$ is measured before decision
$t$. This selector proof needs no maximal-coupling kernel. Applying
$|\E_\mu f-\E_\nu f|\le
\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)\operatorname{span}(f)$ to $g_{H-t}$ bounds the
occupancy term. Exact centering gives
\[
\widehat L_{H,\pi}(\pi_\alpha)-\eta_H(\pi)
=\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\E_{s\sim d_t^\pi}[g_{H-t}(s)+\beta_{H-t}(s)].
\]
Subtracting proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_cpi_exact_coupling}; moving absolute
values inside the current-occupancy expectation proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_defect_upper_bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform finite-horizon CPI specialization]
\label{cor:finite_horizon_cpi_uniform}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_horizon_cpi}, define
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI},h}
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal M_h}|g_h(s)|,
&
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal M_h}|\beta_h(s)|.
\label{eq:fh_candidate_bias}
\end{align}
Then $\Delta_h\le2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI},h}$ and
\begin{align}
\eta_H(\pi_\alpha)
\ge{}&\widehat L_{H,\pi}(\pi_\alpha)
-\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},H-t}
\notag\\
&-2\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI},H-t}[1-(1-\alpha)^t].
\label{eq:fh_cpi_scalar}
\end{align}
For uniform bounds
$\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}\le\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI},h}\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}$, define
\begin{equation}
G_H(q):=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}q^t=\frac{1-q^H}{1-q},
\qquad 0\le q<1.
\label{eq:fh_GS_def}
\end{equation}
The total penalty in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_cpi_scalar} is at most
\begin{align}
\alpha\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}G_H(\gamma)
+2\alpha\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}
[G_H(\gamma)-G_H(\gamma(1-\alpha))].
\label{eq:fh_cpi_uniform_exact}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Stagewise policy-overlap refinement]
\label{cor:finite_horizon_overlap}
Fix a finite evaluator depth $q$ and bounded measurable approximations
$U_{h,q}$ on the mixture domains. Write
\[
E_h:=\|U_{h,q}-V_h^\pi\|_{\infty,\mathcal M_h},
\qquad E_0=0,
\]
where $U_{0,q}=V_0^\pi=b$ on
$\mathcal M_0=\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$.
Define
\[
\tau_h(s):=\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}(\cdot\mid s),\pi_h(\cdot\mid s)\right),
\qquad
\tau_{h,\infty}:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal M_h}\tau_h(s).
\]
Suppose $\widehat A_h$ is the exactly centered one-step estimator derived
from $U_{h-1,q}$, with reward and transition expectations evaluated exactly.
Then
\begin{align}
|\beta_h(s)|
&\le2\gamma E_{h-1}\tau_h(s),
\label{eq:fh_overlap_pointwise}\\
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}
&\le2\gamma E_{h-1}\tau_{h,\infty},
\label{eq:fh_overlap_uniform}\\
\E_{s\sim d_{H-h}^\pi}|\beta_h(s)|
&\le2\gamma E_{h-1}
\E_{s\sim d_{H-h}^\pi}\tau_h(s).
\label{eq:fh_overlap_average}
\end{align}
In particular, the common terminal boundary gives $E_0=0$, so the stage-$1$
advantage-estimation defect is zero.

More generally, let $\ell_h=\min\{K,h\}$ and define the partial-block
estimator
\begin{align}
\widehat Q_{h,q}^{(\ell_h)}(s,a)
:=\E\!\left[
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell_h-1}\gamma^i r_i
+\gamma^{\ell_h}U_{h-\ell_h,q}(S_{\ell_h})
\,\middle|\,
\substack{S_0=s,\ A_0=a;\\
A_i\sim\pi_{h-i}(\cdot\mid S_i),\ 1\le i<\ell_h}
\right],
\label{eq:fh_kstep_qhat}
\end{align}
and center it exactly under $\pi_h$. Its candidate defect satisfies
\begin{align}
|\beta_h^{(\ell_h)}(s)|
&\le2\gamma^{\ell_h}E_{h-\ell_h}\tau_h(s),
\label{eq:fh_overlap_kstep_pointwise}\\
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}^{(\ell_h)}
&\le2\gamma^{\ell_h}E_{h-\ell_h}\tau_{h,\infty}.
\label{eq:fh_overlap_kstep_uniform}
\end{align}
Thus a final partial block with $K>h$ has $\ell_h=h$ and zero bootstrap
error because $E_0=0$.

If the residual theorem is used to certify $E_h$, its Bellman domains must
contain the mixture domains $\mathcal M_h$ and satisfy the corresponding
invariance assumptions. A one-deviation closure alone is insufficient. Under
those enlarged-domain premises, take the estimator depth $q=n<m$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fh_finite_reference} gives
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}
&\le2\gamma\tau_{h,\infty}
\left[D_{h-1;n,m}
+\sum_{j=0}^{J_{h-1}-1}
\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-1-jK,m}\right],
\label{eq:fh_overlap_finite_reference}
\end{align}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:fh_finite_depth_residual} gives
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}
&\le2\gamma\tau_{h,\infty}
\sum_{j=0}^{J_{h-1}-1}
\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-1-jK,n}.
\label{eq:fh_overlap_finite_depth}
\end{align}
For an $\ell_h$-step estimator, the corresponding explicit bounds are
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}^{(\ell_h)}
&\le2\gamma^{\ell_h}\tau_{h,\infty}
\left[D_{h-\ell_h;n,m}
+\sum_{j=0}^{J_{h-\ell_h}-1}
\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-\ell_h-jK,m}\right],
\label{eq:fh_overlap_kstep_finite_reference}\\
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},h}^{(\ell_h)}
&\le2\gamma^{\ell_h}\tau_{h,\infty}
\sum_{j=0}^{J_{h-\ell_h}-1}
\gamma^{jK}\epsilon_{h-\ell_h-jK,n}.
\label{eq:fh_overlap_kstep_finite_depth}
\end{align}
The fallback follows from $\tau_h\le1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_{h-1}=U_{h-1,q}-V_{h-1}^\pi$ and
$q_e(s,a)=\gamma\E[e_{h-1}(S_1)\mid s,a]$. Exact centering gives
$\beta_h=\E_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand},h}}q_e-\E_{\pi_h}q_e$.
Recursive invariance gives a common action set of full current- and
candidate-policy measure on which
$q_e(s,a)\in[-\gamma E_{h-1},\gamma E_{h-1}]$. The TV expectation inequality
on this essential range proves
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fh_overlap_pointwise}--\eqref{eq:fh_overlap_average}.
For the partial block, the reward terms cancel and leave
$q_e^{(\ell_h)}(s,a)=\gamma^{\ell_h}
\E[e_{h-\ell_h}(S_{\ell_h})\mid s,a]$, which lies in
$[-\gamma^{\ell_h}E_{h-\ell_h},
\gamma^{\ell_h}E_{h-\ell_h}]$ on the analogous common essential action
range. Substituting the two finite-horizon residual bounds proves the final
displays.
\end{proof}

For $H=0$, $\eta_0(\pi)=b$, $G_0=0$, and every sum and penalty is empty. At
$h=0$, values equal the common boundary and no advantage is needed; set
$g_0=\Delta_0=0$ if convenient. When $K>h$, the last backup is the partial
block $\ell_h=h$ and its terminal error vanishes. When $\gamma=0$, only the
$t=0$ reward remains and the occupancy-shift penalty is zero. At $\alpha=0$
every policy-shift term vanishes; at $\alpha=1$ the selector bound is zero at
$t=0$ and one for $t\ge1$. A nonzero common boundary cancels exactly in every
policy comparison. In a folded zero-bootstrap representation, the terminal
tail is already included in the folded reward and must not be added again.

\begin{theorem}[First-mismatch deployment perturbation]
\label{thm:deployment_perturbation}
Fix $0\le\gamma<1$ and
$\pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$ for
$\alpha\in[0,1]$. Let $\widetilde\pi$ be a deployed stochastic kernel in the
same fixed MDP as $\pi_\alpha$, with the same initial law $\rho$. Assume the
state and action spaces are standard Borel, and let
$\mathsf K(dr,ds'\mid s,a)$ be the measurable joint reward-transition
kernel. The policy kernels then admit a measurable statewise maximal-coupling
kernel.

Let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$ be measurable with
$\rho(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}})=1$, and assume the policy-induced transition
kernels of both $\pi_\alpha$ and $\widetilde\pi$ leave
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$ invariant. For every
$s\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$, assume $\mathsf K$ assigns rewards in
$[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$ for $\pi_\alpha(da\mid s)$- and
$\widetilde\pi(da\mid s)$-almost every action. Write
$\Delta_r:=r_{\max}-r_{\min}$ and suppose
\begin{equation}
\sup_{s\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}}
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\widetilde\pi(\cdot\mid s),\pi_\alpha(\cdot\mid s)\right)
\le\delta_{\mathrm{dep}},
\qquad \delta_{\mathrm{dep}}\in[0,1].
\label{eq:deployment_uniform_tv}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\left|\eta(\widetilde\pi)-\eta(\pi_\alpha)\right|
\le
\frac{\Delta_r\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})}.
\label{eq:deployment_tv_bound}
\end{equation}
The extra denominator factor satisfies
$1-\gamma+\gamma\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}\ge1-\gamma$, so the first-mismatch
constant improves the generic perturbation constant.

For an integer horizon $H\ge0$, impose the same assumptions on the
clock-complete deployment domain at every remaining horizon. If both returns
use the same scalar terminal boundary $b$, then
\begin{align}
\left|\eta_H(\widetilde\pi)-\eta_H(\pi_\alpha)\right|
&\le
\Delta_r\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t
\left[1-(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})^{t+1}\right]
\notag\\
&=
\Delta_r\left[
G_H(\gamma)-(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})
G_H\!\left(\gamma(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:deployment_tv_finite_horizon}
\end{align}
The common term $\gamma^H b$ cancels exactly, including when $b\ne0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two processes from the same initial state. Whenever their states
agree at decision $t$, use a measurable maximal coupling of
$\pi_\alpha(\cdot\mid s_t)$ and $\widetilde\pi(\cdot\mid s_t)$. Conditional
on a matched history, their actions agree with probability at least
$1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}$. When the actions agree, draw one shared reward and
successor state from $\mathsf K(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$. After the first action
mismatch, use any measurable coupling.

Let $E_t$ be the event that the actions agree at every decision
$0,\ldots,t$. Iterated conditioning gives
\[
\Pr(E_t)\ge(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})^{t+1}.
\]
The exponent is $t+1$ because the reward at time $t$ follows decision $t$.
On $E_t$, the two rewards at time $t$ are identical. Since both rewards lie
in an interval of width $\Delta_r$,
\[
\left|\E[\widetilde r_t]-\E[r_t^\alpha]\right|
\le\Delta_r\Pr(E_t^c)
\le\Delta_r\left[1-(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})^{t+1}\right].
\]
Summing proves the finite-horizon bound; the common terminal scalar cancels.
Letting $H\to\infty$ is valid because rewards are bounded, and
\begin{align*}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t
\left[1-(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})^{t+1}\right]
&=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
-\frac{1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}}
{1-\gamma(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})}\\
&=\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})}.
\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Attainment by a first-mismatch process]
\label{rem:deployment_attainment}
The constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation} are attained by a
two-state MDP. Let $\ell$ be initial and $u$ absorbing. At $\ell$, action
$a_0$ gives reward $0$ and returns to $\ell$, while $a_1$ gives reward
$\Delta_r$ and enters $u$; every action at $u$ gives reward $\Delta_r$ and
remains there. Let $\pi_\alpha$ choose $a_0$ at $\ell$, and let
$\widetilde\pi$ choose $a_1$ with probability $\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}$ and
$a_0$ otherwise. Let the policies agree at $u$. The reward difference at
time $t$ is exactly
$\Delta_r[1-(1-\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})^{t+1}]$, so equality holds in both
bounds, with terminal boundary $b=0$ in the finite-horizon construction.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Either-direction uniform-KL deployment bound]
\label{cor:deployment_pinsker}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation}, suppose either
\begin{align*}
\sup_{s\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\widetilde\pi(\cdot\mid s)\|\pi_\alpha(\cdot\mid s)\right)
&\le\kappa,
\quad\text{or}\\
\sup_{s\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\pi_\alpha(\cdot\mid s)\|\widetilde\pi(\cdot\mid s)\right)
&\le\kappa
\end{align*}
for a finite $\kappa$. Define
\begin{equation}
\bar\delta_\kappa:=\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{2}}\right\}.
\label{eq:deployment_pinsker_radius}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\left|\eta(\widetilde\pi)-\eta(\pi_\alpha)\right|
\le
\frac{\Delta_r\bar\delta_\kappa}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\bar\delta_\kappa)},
\label{eq:deployment_kl_bound}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\left|\eta_H(\widetilde\pi)-\eta_H(\pi_\alpha)\right|
\le
\Delta_r\left[
G_H(\gamma)-(1-\bar\delta_\kappa)
G_H\!\left(\gamma(1-\bar\delta_\kappa)\right)
\right].
\label{eq:deployment_kl_finite_horizon}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Pinsker's inequality in either direction and $\operatorname{TV}\le1$ show
that the actual uniform TV discrepancy is at most $\bar\delta_\kappa$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation} with
$\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}=\bar\delta_\kappa$; both right-hand sides are
increasing in this radius. If
the selected KL divergence is infinite, this corollary does not apply; the TV
theorem remains available when Eq.~\eqref{eq:deployment_uniform_tv} is
established directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Occupancy-aware CPI with deployment discrepancy]
\label{cor:combined_cpi_deployment}
Assume Theorems~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} and
\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation} hold in one fixed MDP with one initial law.
Let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:mixture_deployment_domains}, and let
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$ be measurable, carry the same initial law, and
be recursively invariant under both $\pi_\alpha$ and $\widetilde\pi$.
Assume the standard-Borel, bounded-reward, and uniform-TV conditions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_perturbation}. For any established one-sided
bound $B_\alpha\ge\Xi_\alpha$,
\begin{align}
\eta(\widetilde\pi)
\ge{}&
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-\frac{B_\alpha}{1-\gamma}
-\frac{\gamma\alpha^2\Delta_g}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}
\notag\\
&-
\frac{\Delta_r\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\delta_{\mathrm{dep}})}.
\label{eq:combined_cpi_deployment}
\end{align}
A distilled, logit-interpolated, hidden-state-interpolated,
parameter-interpolated, or otherwise realized policy is covered only when it
acts in this same MDP and its TV or KL discrepancy from the exact
probability-space mixture is established uniformly on
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dep}}$. For persistent state $(x,y,z,h)$, all policies
must use the same frozen recurrent map that determines the carried-latent
transition. Changing that map changes the MDP.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lower-bound $\eta(\widetilde\pi)$ by $\eta(\pi_\alpha)$ minus
Eq.~\eqref{eq:deployment_tv_bound}, then apply
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem_occupancy} with $\Xi_\alpha\le B_\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Boundary cases and uniform scope]
\label{rem:deployment_uniform_scope}
At $\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}=0$, both deployment penalties are zero. At
$\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}=1$, they are $\Delta_r/(1-\gamma)$ and
$\Delta_rG_H(\gamma)$. At $\gamma=0$ and $H\ge1$, both reduce to
$\Delta_r\delta_{\mathrm{dep}}$; at $H=0$, the finite-horizon penalty is zero.

The residual, centering, TV, and KL premises are uniform on their declared
domains. Replay-batch means, batch maxima, held-out finite maxima, and local
finite-difference proxies are diagnostics. They do not establish the uniform
premises.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of the Occupancy-Aware CPI Result}
\label{app:cpi_derivation}

The proof first corrects the estimated surrogate by its signed error, then
bounds the change from base to mixture occupancy. It uses the
performance-difference identity~\citep{KakadeLangford2002} and the Bernoulli
selector calculation in Lemma~\ref{lem:exact_mixture_occupancy}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error}]
For the true surrogate
\[
L_\pi(\pi'):=\eta(\pi)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\pi,\,a\sim\pi'(\cdot\mid s)}[A^\pi(s,a)],
\]
linearity of the exact mixture and
$\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}A^\pi(s,a)=0$ give
\begin{align*}
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)-L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
&=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d_\pi}
\left[(1-\alpha)c(s)+\alpha b(s)\right]\\
&=\frac{\Xi_\alpha}{1-\gamma},
\end{align*}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:surrogate_signed_identity}.

The performance-difference identity also gives
\begin{align*}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)-L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
&=\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
\left(\E_{d_{\pi_\alpha}}g-\E_{d_\pi}g\right).
\end{align*}
For any bounded measurable $g$ and probability measures $p,q$,
$|\E_p g-\E_q g|\le\operatorname{span}(g)\operatorname{TV}(p,q)$.
Applying this inequality on $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and then
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact_mixture_occupancy} yields
\[
\eta(\pi_\alpha)-L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
\ge-
\frac{\gamma\alpha^2\Delta_g}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\]
Combining the last display with the exact surrogate identity proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem_occupancy}. Any $B_\alpha\ge\Xi_\alpha$ may replace
the signed term. Under exact centering, $c=0$ and
$\Xi_\alpha=\alpha\E_{d_\pi}b\le
\alpha\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}$. Finally,
$\Delta_g\le2\sup_s|g(s)|=2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}$, proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:cpi_bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Occupancy-averaged centering and candidate defects]
\label{cor:cpi_centering_defect}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error}, let
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cent},d}
:=\E_{s\sim d_\pi}|c(s)|,
\qquad
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},d}
:=\E_{s\sim d_\pi}|b(s)|.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
\ge
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-\frac{(1-\alpha)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cent},d}
+\alpha\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},d}}{1-\gamma}
-\frac{\gamma\alpha^2\Delta_g}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:cpi_centering_defect_bound}
\end{equation}
Pointwise uniform bounds on $|c|$ and $|b|$ imply the corresponding looser
version immediately. For a value-derived estimator,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:candidate_overlap_average} supplies
$\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},d}\le
2\gamma\delta_V\E_{d_\pi}\tau(s)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The choice
$B_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cent},d}
+\alpha\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand},d}$
upper-bounds $\Xi_\alpha$. Substitute it in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem_occupancy}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Safe-step boundary cases}
\label{app:safe_step_boundaries}

For Corollary~\ref{cor:one_step_improvement_condition}, the zero step
$\alpha=0$ is equality. At $\alpha=1$, the bound certifies the full candidate
step exactly when $M_d\ge\gamma\Delta_g$. The remaining nonredundant cases
are:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\gamma=0$ and $M_d\ge0$, every $\alpha\in[0,1]$ is certified;
\item if $M_d<0$, no positive step is certified by this bound;
\item if $\gamma>0$, $M_d=0$, and $\Delta_g>0$, no positive step is certified;
\item if $M_d=\Delta_g=0$, every step has a zero lower bound;
\item if $\gamma>0$, $M_d>0$, and $M_d\ge\gamma\Delta_g$, every
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ is certified;
\item if $\gamma>0$ and $0<M_d<\gamma\Delta_g$, every
\begin{equation}
\alpha\le
\frac{M_d(1-\gamma)}{\gamma(\Delta_g-M_d)}
\label{eq:explicit_safe_step}
\end{equation}
is certified. The denominator is positive and the threshold lies strictly
between zero and one.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:one_step_improvement_condition}]
The invariance of $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ under both endpoint policies
implies its invariance under every $\pi_\alpha$. Hence
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} applies for each
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ with the same choice
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{mix}}=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$, the same
$\Delta_g$, estimator, defect function, and one-sided defect bound.
Exact centering removes the current-policy component:
\[
\E_{s\sim d_\pi,\,a\sim\pi_\alpha(\cdot\mid s)}\widehat A(s,a)
=\alpha\widehat g.
\]
Together with the definition of the fixed candidate defect $b$, this gives
the candidate-slope identity required by the certificate,
\begin{equation}
\widehat g
=\E_{s\sim d_\pi}[g(s)+b(s)].
\label{eq:ghat_signed_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\E_{d_\pi}g
=\widehat g-\E_{d_\pi}b
\ge \widehat g-\overline{\beta}_d
=M_d.
\label{eq:g_current_lower_bound}
\end{equation}
The performance-difference identity, the span--TV expectation inequality,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:exact_mixture_occupancy} now give
\[
\eta(\pi_\alpha)-\eta(\pi)
=\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}\E_{d_{\pi_\alpha}}g
\ge\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
\left[
\E_{d_\pi}g
-\frac{\gamma\alpha\Delta_g}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}
\right]
\ge\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
\left[M_d-\frac{\gamma\alpha\Delta_g}
{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}\right].
\]
This proves Eqs.~\eqref{eq:safe_step_interval_bound}
and~\eqref{eq:sufficient_improvement_condition}. The exact signed choice
meets Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta_d_bound} with equality, while
$\E b\le\E|b|\le\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}$ proves the other two
specializations. For
\[
\varphi(\alpha)
:=\frac{\gamma\alpha\Delta_g}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha},
\qquad
\varphi'(\alpha)
=\frac{\gamma\Delta_g(1-\gamma)}
{(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)^2}\ge0.
\]
Thus $\varphi(0)=0$ and
$\max_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\varphi(\alpha)=\varphi(1)=\gamma\Delta_g$.
If $\gamma>0$ and $0<M_d<\gamma\Delta_g$, then
$M_d<\Delta_g$ because $\gamma<1$, so $\Delta_g-M_d>0$ and the threshold is
positive. Moreover,
\[
\frac{M_d(1-\gamma)}{\gamma(\Delta_g-M_d)}<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M_d<\gamma\Delta_g,
\]
which is exactly the branch premise. Rearrangement therefore gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:explicit_safe_step} directly, with no outer minimum or further
intersection with $[0,1]$. The monotonicity and direct
substitution give every stated boundary case. In particular,
$\varphi(1)=\gamma\Delta_g$ places
$M_d=\gamma\Delta_g$ in the all-step branch, and when
$M_d=\Delta_g=0$ the displayed lower bound is zero for every step.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Residual-to-CPI substitutions and proof}
\label{app:proof_finite_depth_certificate}

\begin{corollary}[Self-bootstrap and target-network residual certificates]
\label{cor:finite_depth_residual_certificate}
Use the measurable Bellman domain of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}
with $\mathcal C=\mathcal R_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$.
Take $U_n\in B_b(\mathcal C)$ and use the common scalar boundary
$U_n(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=V^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=b$. Define
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}
\triangleq
\|U_n-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_n\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:eps_res_n_def}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:finite_depth_residual_certificate}
\end{equation}
If $\widehat A=\widehat A_{U_n}$ is the exact one-step centered estimator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}, with its transition expectation and baseline sum
under $\pi$ evaluated exactly, then Assumption~\ref{ass:Ahat_error_bound}
holds with
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_A
\triangleq
\frac{2\gamma\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:finite_depth_residual_adv}
\end{equation}
Consequently, under Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error},
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
\ge
\widehat{L}_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-
\frac{2\alpha\gamma\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}\,
\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}}{1-\gamma^K}
-
\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:finite_depth_residual_cpi}
\end{equation}

For a finite depth $q\ge0$, instead suppose
Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge} holds on the same domain with
$\mathcal E=\mathcal C$. Then
\begin{equation}
\|U_q-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\frac{R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:target_network_value_certificate}
\end{equation}
For finite depths $0\le n<m$, if $U_n,U_m\in B_b(\mathcal C)$ use the
same scalar boundary and the depth-$m$ target-network quantities are
established, then
\begin{equation}
\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\|U_n-U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\frac{R_{\mathrm{targ},m}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:target_network_finite_reference}
\end{equation}
For the depth-$q$ target statement, take
$\widehat A=\widehat A_{U_q}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}, with its
transition expectation and current-policy baseline evaluated exactly, and
define $\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsA0_def}. Then
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}
\le
2\gamma\tau_\infty\frac{R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}.
\label{eq:target_network_candidate_bias}
\end{equation}
Under the exact-mixture and scalar-span hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error},
\begin{align}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
&\ge
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\tau_\infty}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma^K)}R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}
-\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)},
\label{eq:target_network_cpi}\\
\eta(\pi_\alpha)
&\ge
\widehat L_\pi(\pi_\alpha)
-\frac{2\alpha\gamma\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}
\left(
\|U_n-U_m\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\frac{R_{\mathrm{targ},m}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}
\right)
-\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:target_network_finite_reference_cpi}
\end{align}
The second display uses the same exact value-derived construction at depth
$n$. Every target expression retains the propagated-lag quantity
$R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}$ before the ordinary sup-lag fallback
$R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}\le R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{\sup}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because $\mathcal{T}^\pi_K$ is a $\gamma^K$-contraction with fixed point $V^\pi$,
\begin{align*}
\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le
\|U_n-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_n\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+
\|\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_n-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}} \\
&\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}
+
\gamma^K\|U_n-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\end{align*}
Rearranging gives~\eqref{eq:finite_depth_residual_certificate}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app} yields~\eqref{eq:finite_depth_residual_adv}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap} sharpens the candidate bias to
$2\gamma\tau_\infty\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}/(1-\gamma^K)$.
Substitution into Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_depth_residual_cpi}; $\tau_\infty\le1$ recovers the
uniform fallback.

For the target-network forms, Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge}
and the same Bellman contraction argument give the following, where
$\|\cdot\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ abbreviates
$\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ within this
proof:
\[
(1-\gamma^K)\|U_q-V^\pi\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\|U_q-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_q\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P},
\]
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_value_certificate}. Adding and
subtracting $U_m$ and applying this result at $q=m$ proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_finite_reference}. For the stated exact
value-derived estimator, Corollary~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap} applies with
$\delta_V\le R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P}/(1-\gamma^K)$, proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_candidate_bias}. Substitution into the scalar
specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_cpi}. Applying the same overlap result to the
depth-$n$ estimator with
\[
\delta_V\le
\|U_n-U_m\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\frac{R_{\mathrm{targ},m}^{P}}{1-\gamma^K}
\]
proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_finite_reference_cpi}. No step identifies
the target-network population operator with $\mathcal T_K^\pi$; the former
only supplies the displayed upper bound on the latter's residual.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Self-bootstrap residual to fixed-reference residual]
\label{rem:residual_bridge}
The uniform self-bootstrap residual below upper-bounds
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*$ up to the same truncation gap:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*
\le
\|U_n-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_n\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+
(1+\gamma^K)\|U_n-U_*\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\label{eq:residual_bridge}
\end{equation}
This follows by adding and subtracting $U_n$ inside
$U_*-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_*$ and using $K$-step Bellman contraction on the
final term. It is distinct from the target-network population residual in
Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge};
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_residual_bridge} controls the self-bootstrap
residual only after also controlling the propagated target-network lag
$\|P_\pi^K(\bar V-U_n)\|_\infty$; the ordinary sup-lag
$\|\bar V-U_n\|_\infty$ is the nonexpansive fallback. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} with
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and the Lipschitz-head condition on that
domain, combining \eqref{eq:residual_bridge} with
Proposition~\ref{prop:banach} gives
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*
\le
\|U_n-\mathcal{T}^\pi_K U_n\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+
(1+\gamma^K)\,L_V\,\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,C_z.
\label{eq:residual_bridge_trunc}
\end{equation}
Finite-batch or mean-square estimates of either residual remain diagnostics
and do not establish these sup-norm inequalities.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Centering and baseline computation]

\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A(s,a)] = 0 \qquad \text{for all } s.
\]
This holds identically when $\widehat A$ is defined as in~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app} with the baseline computed exactly as
$B_V(s)=\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat Q_V(s,b)]$ (e.g., by summation over a finite action space).

If the baseline is approximated, centering may fail. We quantify this by the \emph{centering defect}
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cent}}
:=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_\pi}\left|\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}[\widehat A(s,a)]\right|.
\]
The exact-centered scalar specialization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem}
corresponds to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cent}}=0$; the signed master inequality
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_theorem_occupancy} also covers nonzero centering defects.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Value to one-step advantage error]
\label{lem:A_error_app}
Let $\mathcal{R}_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ be the advantage-evaluation closure from Definition~\ref{def:adv_closure},
and let $\delta_V := \|V-V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$.
Then for every $s\in\mathcal C_\pi$ and for
$\pi(da\mid s)$- and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(da\mid s)$-almost every $a$,
\[
|\widehat A_V(s,a) - A^\pi(s,a)| \le 2\gamma\,\delta_V.
\]
\end{lemma}

The factor $2\gamma$ is tight worst-case because error appears in both $Q$ and the baseline.
\begin{remark}[Extensions to $K$-step and GAE-style advantages]
\label{rem:A_error_kstep_app}
Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app} is stated for the \emph{one-step} estimator~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}, hence the factor $2\gamma$.
If instead one defines a $K$-step action-value estimate
\begin{equation*}
\widehat Q_V^{(K)}(s,a) :=
\E\biggl[\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\gamma^t r_t + \gamma^K V(s_K) \biggm| s_0=s, a_0=a, \pi\biggr],
\end{equation*}
and the corresponding centered advantage
$\widehat A_V^{(K)}(s,a):=\widehat Q_V^{(K)}(s,a)-\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[\widehat Q_V^{(K)}(s,b)]$
(with the baseline computed exactly), then the same argument yields
\[
|\widehat A_V^{(K)}(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)| \le 2\gamma^K\,\delta_V.
\]
$\lambda$-return or GAE-style estimators lie outside the displayed bridge unless their estimator-specific uniform error is separately established. Such an error may be supplied directly as $\varepsilon_A$ in Assumption~\ref{ass:Ahat_error_bound}; no GAE coefficient is derived here.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Advantage error in terms of theorem parameters]
\label{cor:epsA_dials_app}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} and the exact-centering conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app},
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_A\le 2\gamma B_{n,m}.
\end{equation}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} with
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and the Lipschitz-head condition on that
domain, taking $m\to\infty$ yields
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_A
&\le
\underbrace{\frac{2\gamma\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*}{1-\gamma^K}}_{\text{architectural}}
+
\underbrace{2\gamma\,L_V\,\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,C_z}_{\text{unrolling}}.
\label{eq:epsA_dials_app}
\end{align}
Here $\varepsilon_A$ bounds the advantage error on $\mathcal C_\pi$ for
$\pi(da\mid s)$- and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(da\mid s)$-almost every action,
and the intermediate value-error quantity in Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app} is taken over the advantage-evaluation closure $\mathcal{R}_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ (Definition~\ref{def:adv_closure}).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Lemma~\ref{lem:interior_proj} (Interior projection fixed points)}

\begin{lemma}[Interior projection fixed points]
\label{lem:interior_proj}
Let $\Pi_R:\R^d\to\R^d$ be the Euclidean projection onto the closed ball $\{z:\|z\|_2\le R\}$ as in~\eqref{eq:projection}.
Suppose $z^*=\Pi_R(u)$ for some $u\in\R^d$.
If $\|z^*\|_2 < R$, then $z^* = u$ (i.e., the projection is inactive at $u$).
Consequently, if $z^*$ is a fixed point of $\widetilde f_\theta(z,y,x)=\Pi_R(f_\theta(z,y,x))$ and $\|z^*\|_2 < R$, then $f_\theta(z^*,y,x)=z^*$ and $\Pi_R$ is inactive at $f_\theta(z^*,y,x)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:projection}, if $\|u\|_2 \le R$ then $\Pi_R(u)=u$.
Since $z^*=\Pi_R(u)$ lies in the open ball $\|z^*\|_2<R$, we have $z^*\ne R\tfrac{u}{\|u\|_2}$, so the second case of~\eqref{eq:projection} cannot apply; hence $\|u\|_2\le R$ and $\Pi_R(u)=u=z^*$.
For the consequence: if $z^*=\widetilde f_\theta(z^*,y,x)=\Pi_R(f_\theta(z^*,y,x))$ and $\|z^*\|_2<R$, applying the first statement with $u=f_\theta(z^*,y,x)$ gives $f_\theta(z^*,y,x)=z^*$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:banach}}

\begin{proof}
Fix $s\in\mathcal D^\circ$.
By Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip}, $T_s$ maps
$\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{inv}}$ to itself (forward invariance) and is a
contraction with modulus $L_z<1$.
Since $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{inv}}$ is a closed subset of the complete metric space $(\ZZ,\|\cdot\|)$, it is itself complete.

Banach's theorem yields a unique fixed point
$z^*(s)\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{inv}}$ and linear
convergence~\eqref{eq:linear_convergence}.

Under the additional measurability premises in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint_measurable_recurrence}, the iterates are measurable by
induction. Indeed, if $z^{(j)}$ is measurable, then
$s\mapsto(s,z^{(j)}(s))$ is measurable into the product space, so
$z^{(j+1)}(s)=T(s,z^{(j)}(s))$ is measurable. Banach convergence gives
$z^{(j)}(s)\to z^*(s)$ pointwise. For every nonempty closed
$F\subseteq\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$, continuity of distance and pointwise
convergence give
\[
d(z^*(s),F)=\lim_{j\to\infty}d(z^{(j)}(s),F).
\]
The right side is a pointwise limit of measurable real functions, and
\[
\{s:z^*(s)\in F\}=\{s:d(z^*(s),F)=0\}.
\]
The empty closed set has empty preimage. Since closed sets generate the Borel
$\sigma$-algebra, $z^*$ is measurable.

For the finite-unrolling bound, specialize to
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and take
$z^{(0)}(s)=z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))$:
\[
z^*(s) - z^{(n)}(s)
= \sum_{k=n}^{\infty}(z^{(k+1)}(s) - z^{(k)}(s)).
\]
Contraction gives
$\|z^{(k+1)}(s)-z^{(k)}(s)\|
\le L_z^k\|z^{(1)}(s)-z^{(0)}(s)\|$, so
\[
\|z^*(s)-z^{(n)}(s)\|
\le \|z^{(1)}(s)-z^{(0)}(s)\| \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} L_z^k
= \frac{L_z^n}{1-L_z}\|z^{(1)}(s)-z^{(0)}(s)\|.
\]
By definition~\eqref{eq:Cz_def}, using the declared update
$\widetilde f_\theta$,
\[
\|z^{(1)}(s)-z^{(0)}(s)\|
=\|T_s(z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s)))-z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\|
\le C_z.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:drift_bound}}

\begin{proof}
For $T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}$ a $\kappa_n$-contraction (where $\kappa_n = L_z^n$):
\begin{align*}
\|z_{t+1} - z^*(x,y_t)\| &= \|T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}(z_t) - T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}(z^*(x,y_t))\| \le \kappa_n e_t.
\end{align*}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:twotimescale}:
\[
\|z^*(x,y_t) - z^*(x,y_{t+1})\| \le L_{z^*}\|y_{t+1}-y_t\|_{\mathcal{E}} \le L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}.
\]
Thus:
\[
e_{t+1} = \|z_{t+1} - z^*(x,y_{t+1})\| \le \|z_{t+1} - z^*(x,y_t)\| + \|z^*(x,y_t) - z^*(x,y_{t+1})\| \le \kappa_n e_t + L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}.
\]

Unrolling this recurrence:
\[
e_t \le \kappa_n^t e_0 + L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max} \sum_{j=0}^{t-1} \kappa_n^j = \kappa_n^t e_0 + \frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}(1 - \kappa_n^t)}{1-\kappa_n}.
\]

Using $e_0 \le E_0$ and noting that this expression is maximized either at $t=0$ (giving $E_0$) or as $t \to \infty$ (giving $\frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}$), we have:
\[
\sup_t e_t \le \max\left(E_0, \frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}\right) \le E_0 + \frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}.
\]

For the used latent:
\[
\|z_t^{(n)} - z^*(x,y_t)\| = \|T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}(z_t) - z^*(x,y_t)\|
= \|T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}(z_t) - T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}(z^*(x,y_t))\| \le \kappa_n e_t,
\]
where the second equality uses that $z^*(x,y_t)$ is the fixed point of $T_{x,y_t}$,
hence also of $T_{x,y_t}^{\circ n}$, and the inequality follows from
$\kappa_n$-contractivity of the $n$-fold composition.
Thus:
\[
\sup_t \|z_t^{(n)} - z^*(x,y_t)\|
\le
\kappa_n \sup_t e_t
\le
\kappa_n \max\!\left(E_0,\,\frac{L_{z^*}\Delta y_{\max}}{1-\kappa_n}\right)
=
\Cdrift(n),
\]
and the final ``$\le$'' in~\eqref{eq:Cdrift_def} gives the simpler additive upper bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth}}

\begin{proof}
Write $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ for the ordinary sup-norm on
$\mathcal C$. Invariance and the Markov-kernel measurability property imply
$P_\pi^K V\in B_b(\mathcal C)$ whenever $V\in B_b(\mathcal C)$; bounded
$r_K^\pi$ therefore makes $\mathcal T_K^\pi$ a self-map. For
$V,W\in B_b(\mathcal C)$,
\[
\|\mathcal T_K^\pi V-\mathcal T_K^\pi W\|_\infty
\le\gamma^K\|V-W\|_\infty.
\]
The bounded-reward premise makes the ordinary discounted policy value
well defined, bounded, and measurable, with the uniformly convergent
representation
\[
V^\pi=\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t P_\pi^t r^\pi.
\]
Splitting this series after $K$ terms gives
$V^\pi=\mathcal T_K^\pi V^\pi$. Completeness and Banach's theorem then
identify this policy value as the unique fixed point of
$\mathcal T_K^\pi$.
Thus
\begin{align*}
\|U_m-V^\pi\|_\infty
&\le \|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_\infty
+\|\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi V^\pi\|_\infty \\
&\le \|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_\infty
+\gamma^K\|U_m-V^\pi\|_\infty.
\end{align*}
Rearranging and then applying the triangle inequality to
$U_n-U_m+U_m-V^\pi$ proves~\eqref{eq:finite_reference_depth}.

For every nonabsorbing $s$, Lipschitzness of the scalar head and the telescoping latent path give
\begin{align*}
|U_n(s)-U_m(s)|
&\le L_V\|z^{(n)}(s)-z^{(m)}(s)\| \\
&\le L_V\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\|z^{(j+1)}(s)-z^{(j)}(s)\|.
\end{align*}
The left side is zero at $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$ because its value is depth
independent. Taking the supremum only over $\mathcal C^\circ$ for the latent
endpoints proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_endpoint_discrepancy}. The triangle
inequality for the telescoping latent path proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_path_length_exact}; using
$\sup_s\sum_j g_j(s)\le\sum_j\sup_s g_j(s)$ proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_path_length}. No absorbing latent is defined.

Under the $\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ invocation of
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip},
\[
\|z^{(j+1)}(s)-z^{(j)}(s)\|
\le L_z^j\|z^{(1)}(s)-z^{(0)}(s)\|
\le L_z^j C_z.
\]
Summing from $j=n$ through $m-1$ yields~\eqref{eq:finite_reference_contracting}. Proposition~\ref{prop:banach} and the Lipschitz head give $\|U_m-U_*\|_\infty\to0$. Moreover,
\begin{align*}
\big|\|U_m-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_m\|_\infty
-\|U_*-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_*\|_\infty\big|
&\le (1+\gamma^K)\|U_m-U_*\|_\infty \to 0.
\end{align*}
Taking $m\to\infty$ therefore proves~\eqref{eq:epsV_dials}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite-$R$ specialization of the truncation constant]
\label{rem:finite_R_Cz}
Under the Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} initialization condition
$\|z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\|_2\le R$ for every
$s\in\mathcal C^\circ$, with
$\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{inv}}=\{z:\|z\|_2\le R\}$, the projection
$\Pi_R$ ensures
$\|T_s(z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s)))\|_2\le R$ on the same domain. Therefore
\[
C_z
=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\|\widetilde f_\theta(z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s)),y(s),x(s))
-z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\|_2
\le 2R.
\]
The contractive specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} therefore gives
\[
\|U_n - V^\pi\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le
\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*}{1-\gamma^K}
+
2L_V\,\frac{L_z^{\,n}}{1-L_z}\,R.
\]
This makes $R$ explicit only through the finite-unrolling term; it does not
identify the dependence of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*$ on $R$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Projection-Active Theory}
\label{app:projection_theory}

\paragraph{Projection-aware CPI penalty (full statement).}
Assume all premises of Proposition~\ref{prop:proj_sat_contraction} on
$\mathcal C^\circ$, including saturation and the represented-initialization
condition, together with the zero-anchor hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:proj_residual_anchor}, and suppose
$\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)<1$. That proposition establishes
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} on $\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$. Combining
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Lz_sat} and~\eqref{eq:eps_res_proj} with $C_z\le 2R$
yields a projection-aware version of~\eqref{eq:main_bound_dials}:
\begin{align}
\eta(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
&\ge
\widehat{L}_\pi(\pi_{\mathrm{new}})
-
\frac{2\alpha\gamma\tau_\infty}{1-\gamma}
\left(
\frac{\varepsilon_0 + (1+\gamma^K)L_V(R)\,R}{1-\gamma^K}
+
2L_V(R)\,\frac{\bigl(\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)\bigr)^n}{1-\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)}\,R
\right)
\notag\\
&\quad-
\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CPI}}\gamma\alpha^2}
{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)}.
\label{eq:main_bound_proj}
\end{align}
Projection affects both the architectural and truncation terms. The displayed
bound does not make $R$ a monotone performance dial because the residual,
head Lipschitz factor, saturation margin, and optimizer trajectory may all
change with the projection regime.

\begin{proposition}[Projection-induced contraction on a saturated annulus]
\label{prop:proj_sat_contraction}
Fix $R>0$, let $B_R:=\{z:\|z\|_2\le R\}$, and, for
$s\in\mathcal C^\circ$, set
$T_s(z):=\Pi_R(f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s)))$. Define the pre-projection
domain-relative Lipschitz constant
\[
L_z^{\mathrm{pre}}(R)
:=
\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\sup_{\substack{z,z'\in B_R\\ z\neq z'}}
\frac{\|f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s))-f_\theta(z',y(s),x(s))\|_2}
{\|z-z'\|_2}.
\]
Assume there exists $\rho_R>R$ such that
\[
\|f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s))\|_2 \ge \rho_R
\qquad
\text{for all }s\in\mathcal C^\circ\text{ and }z\in B_R.
\]
Then, for every $s\in\mathcal C^\circ$ and $z,z'\in B_R$,
\[
\|T_s(z)-T_s(z')\|_2
\;\le\;
\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)\,\|z-z'\|_2,
\qquad
\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)
:=\frac{R}{\rho_R}\,L_z^{\mathrm{pre}}(R).
\]
Projection also gives $T_s(B_R)\subseteq B_R$. Hence, if
$\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)<1$ and
$z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\in B_R$ for every
$s\in\mathcal C^\circ$, then Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} holds with
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$,
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}=B_R$, and
$L_z\le\overline L_z^{\mathrm{sat}}(R)$. This conclusion does not assert
contraction for input pairs not represented in $\mathcal C^\circ$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $u,v$ with $\|u\|_2,\|v\|_2\ge \rho_R>R$,
\[
\|\Pi_R(u)-\Pi_R(v)\|_2
=
R\left\|\frac{u}{\|u\|_2}-\frac{v}{\|v\|_2}\right\|_2.
\]
Using
\[
\left\|\frac{u}{\|u\|_2}-\frac{v}{\|v\|_2}\right\|_2^2
=
\frac{\|u-v\|_2^2-(\|u\|_2-\|v\|_2)^2}{\|u\|_2\|v\|_2}
\le
\frac{\|u-v\|_2^2}{\rho_R^2},
\]
we obtain
\[
\|\Pi_R(u)-\Pi_R(v)\|_2
\le
\frac{R}{\rho_R}\|u-v\|_2.
\]
Fix $s\in\mathcal C^\circ$ and apply this with
$u=f_\theta(z,y(s),x(s))$ and $v=f_\theta(z',y(s),x(s))$ for
$z,z'\in B_R$. Then
\[
\|T_s(z)-T_s(z')\|_2
\le
\frac{R}{\rho_R}\,L_z^{\mathrm{pre}}(R)\,\|z-z'\|_2.
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Projection-aware residual via a state-dependent latent anchor]
\label{prop:proj_residual_anchor}
Fix the discounted MDP, policy pair, $\gamma$, $K$, closure $\mathcal C$, and
common absorber from Corollary~\ref{cor:finite_depth_residual_certificate}.
Require its bounded-function hypotheses, including
$\mathcal T_K^\pi:B_b(\mathcal C)\to B_b(\mathcal C)$. Write
$\mathcal C^\circ=\mathcal C\setminus\{s_{\mathrm{abs}}\}$. Assume
Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip} holds on
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ with
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}=B_R$. Assume
$(s,z)\mapsto T_s(z)$ is jointly measurable on
$\mathcal C^\circ\times B_R$ and
$s\mapsto z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))$ is measurable into $B_R$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:banach} then makes the represented fixed-point map
$z^*$ measurable. Invoke Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded_value_head} with
$\mathcal D^\circ=\mathcal C^\circ$ and
$\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{bd}}=B_R$, and require that the induced evaluator
$U_*(s)=V_\psi(z^*(s),x(s),y(s))$ belongs to $B_b(\mathcal C)$ with
the common absorbing boundary. Fix any measurable anchor
$z_{\mathrm{anc}}:\mathcal C^\circ\to B_R$ and define
\begin{align*}
B_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)&:=V_\psi(z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s),x(s),y(s)),
&&s\in\mathcal C^\circ,\\
B_{\mathrm{anc}}(s_{\mathrm{abs}})&:=-C_{\max},&& \\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{anc}}
&:=
\|B_{\mathrm{anc}}-\mathcal{T}_K^\pi B_{\mathrm{anc}}\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\end{align*}
Assume $B_{\mathrm{anc}}\in B_b(\mathcal C)$ and let
\begin{align*}
D_{\mathrm{anc}}(R)
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\|z^*(s)-z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)\|_2,\\
L_V(R)
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C^\circ}
\sup_{\substack{z,z'\in B_R\\ z\neq z'}}
\frac{|V_\psi(z,x(s),y(s))-V_\psi(z',x(s),y(s))|}{\|z-z'\|_2}.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*
\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{anc}}
\;+\;
(1+\gamma^K)\,L_V(R)\,D_{\mathrm{anc}}(R).
\label{eq:eps_res_anchor}
\end{equation}
In the special case $z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)\equiv0$ on $\mathcal C^\circ$,
\begin{align*}
B_0(s)&:=V_\psi(0,x(s),y(s)),&&s\in\mathcal C^\circ,\\
B_0(s_{\mathrm{abs}})&:=-C_{\max},&& \\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*
&\le
\varepsilon_{0}
\;+
(1+\gamma^K)\,L_V(R)\,R, \\
\varepsilon_0
&:=
\|B_0-\mathcal{T}_K^\pi B_0\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Add and subtract $B_{\mathrm{anc}}$:
\[
U_*-\mathcal{T}_K^\pi U_*
=
(B_{\mathrm{anc}}-\mathcal{T}_K^\pi B_{\mathrm{anc}})
+
(U_*-B_{\mathrm{anc}})
+
\bigl(\mathcal{T}_K^\pi B_{\mathrm{anc}}-\mathcal{T}_K^\pi U_*\bigr).
\]
Taking $\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ and using the
$\gamma^K$-contraction of $\mathcal{T}_K^\pi$ on the difference of its two images gives
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res}}^*
\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{anc}}
\;+\;
(1+\gamma^K)\,\|U_*-B_{\mathrm{anc}}\|_{\infty,\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\]
For every $s\in\mathcal C^\circ$, both $z^*(s)$ and
$z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)$ lie in $B_R$, so
\begin{align*}
|U_*(s)-B_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)|
&=
|V_\psi(z^*(s),x(s),y(s))
-V_\psi(z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s),x(s),y(s))| \\
&\le
L_V(R)\,\|z^*(s)-z_{\mathrm{anc}}(s)\|_2
\;\le\;
L_V(R)\,D_{\mathrm{anc}}(R).
\end{align*}
At the absorbing boundary, $U_*(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=B_{\mathrm{anc}}(s_{\mathrm{abs}})=-C_{\max}$, so the difference is zero.
The zero-anchor specialization uses $D_{\mathrm{anc}}(R)\le R$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app}}

\begin{proof}
For any $(s,a)$,
\[
\widehat Q_V(s,a) - Q^\pi(s,a)
=
\gamma\,\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)}\bigl[V(s')-V^\pi(s')\bigr].
\]
If $s\in\mathcal C_\pi$, Definition~\ref{def:adv_closure} gives
$P(\mathcal{R}_{\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}\mid s,a)=1$
for $\pi(da\mid s)$- and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(da\mid s)$-almost every $a$.
Thus $|V(s')-V^\pi(s')|\le \delta_V$ for transition-almost every $s'$, and hence
$|\widehat Q_V(s,a)-Q^\pi(s,a)|\le \gamma\delta_V$.

Similarly,
\[
\Bigl|\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}\widehat Q_V(s,b)-\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}Q^\pi(s,b)\Bigr|
\le \gamma\delta_V,
\]
since $b\sim\pi(\cdot|s)$ and the same almost-sure closure condition applies.
Subtracting these two bounds yields
$|\widehat A_V(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)|\le 2\gamma\delta_V$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:persistent_cpi_augmented}}

\begin{proof}
The clock-complete state, frozen shared map $F_n$, and specified initial law define one augmented MDP for both action kernels. On its bounded measurable functions, $\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K$ is a $\gamma^K$-contraction in the augmented closure norm. In this proof, $\|\cdot\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ abbreviates $\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\bar\pi,\bar\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}^{\mathrm{adv}}$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
&\le
\|\widetilde U_n-\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_n\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
+\|\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_n-\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}\\
&\le \bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}
+\gamma^K\|\widetilde U_n-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}.
\end{align*}
Rearrangement proves~\eqref{eq:persistent_augmented_value}. Lemma~\ref{lem:A_error_app}, applied on the augmented closure with exact centering conditional on $\bar s$, gives~\eqref{eq:persistent_augmented_adv}. Corollary~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap} supplies the $\bar\tau_\infty$ refinement. Under the exact pointwise mixture, substitution into Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} proves~\eqref{eq:persistent_augmented_cpi}.

For the target-network statements, add and subtract its population backup on
the same fixed augmented MDP:
\[
\widetilde U_q-\bar{\mathcal T}_K^{\bar\pi}\widetilde U_q
=
\left[
\widetilde U_q-
(\bar r_K^{\bar\pi}+\gamma^K P_{\bar\pi}^K\bar V)
\right]
+\gamma^K P_{\bar\pi}^K(\bar V-\widetilde U_q).
\]
The triangle inequality and nonexpansiveness of $P_{\bar\pi}^K$ prove
Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_residual_bridge}. Bellman contraction then
gives
\[
(1-\gamma^K)
\|\widetilde U_q-V^{\bar\pi}\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}
\le \bar R_{\mathrm{targ},q}^{P},
\]
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_value_certificate}. Adding and
subtracting $\widetilde U_m$ proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_finite_reference}. The augmented version of
Corollary~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap}, applied to the stated exact
value-derived estimator, proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_candidate_bias}.
Substitution into Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error} proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_cpi}; applying the same argument to the
depth-$n$ estimator and Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_finite_reference}
proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:persistent_target_finite_reference_cpi}. Throughout,
$q$ changes only an endpoint function, not $F_n$, the policies, or the
augmented kernel.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the optional slow-drift comparisons follow directly. Lemma~\ref{lem:drift_bound} and the Lipschitz head imply~\eqref{eq:persistent_fixed_comparison}; adding and subtracting $\widetilde U_*$ and applying the residual bound~\eqref{eq:boundA} proves~\eqref{eq:persistent_slow_drift_value}. Finally, the reverse triangle inequality and Bellman contraction give
\begin{align*}
|\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},n}-\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{res},*}|
&\le
\|(\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_*)
-(\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_n-\bar{\mathcal T}^{\bar\pi}_K\widetilde U_*)\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}}\\
&\le(1+\gamma^K)\|\widetilde U_n-\widetilde U_*\|_{\infty}^{\mathrm{adv}},
\end{align*}
which proves~\eqref{eq:persistent_residual_relation}.

\subsection{Algorithm Pseudocode}
\label{app:algorithm_pseudocode}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:upi_app} presents one fixed-base UPI--TRM proposal in
persistent-$z$ form with an explicit remaining-budget clock. The analyzed
object is one proposal at one frozen parameter snapshot; it is not a
recursively promoted policy sequence.
For the episodic-$z$ variant, use $s_k=(x,y_k,h_k)$, omit the carried latent from replay, and reinitialize $z_k \gets z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y_k)$ at the start of each loop iteration.
For a collected segment of length $\ell\le K$, let $d_{\mathrm{seg}}$ indicate
that its final transition terminates. The folded-reward implementation target is
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{fold}}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\gamma^k r_k
+
\mathbf{1}\{\neg d_{\mathrm{seg}}\}\gamma^\ell\bar V(\bar s_\ell).
\label{eq:G_folded_implementation}
\end{equation}
Here $d_{\mathrm{seg}}=0$ implies $\ell=K$; early nonterminal segments are not
admissible exact-$K$ targets. On a terminal segment, the stored final reward is
the formal terminal-transition reward plus $\gamma b$, where
$b=V^\pi(s_{\mathrm{abs}})$. The zero padding and zero terminal bootstrap in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:G_folded_implementation} are therefore an accounting
representation of the formal absorbing tail, not an additional self-loop.
Under frozen-current-policy collection, this target has conditional population
backup $r_K^\pi+\gamma^K P_\pi^K\bar V$ in the episodic model. Hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge} converts its uniform population
residual to the theorem's self-bootstrap residual after adding the propagated
lag $\gamma^K\|P_\pi^K(\bar V-U_n)\|_\infty$. Nonexpansiveness gives the
ordinary sup-lag fallback
$\gamma^K\|\bar V-U_n\|_\infty$. In the persistent model, replace every
Bellman object by its augmented-MDP counterpart: in particular,
$(\mathcal E,P_\pi,U_n,\mathcal T_K^\pi)$ becomes
$(\bar{\mathcal C},P_{\bar\pi},\widetilde U_n,
\bar{\mathcal T}_K^{\bar\pi})$, and the expected-reward term is the augmented
$K$-step expected reward. The sharper lag term is then
$\gamma^K\|P_{\bar\pi}^K(\bar V-\widetilde U_n)\|_\infty$, before the
fallback $\gamma^K\|\bar V-\widetilde U_n\|_\infty$. Empirical regression
loss, population $L^2$ error, and a finite-batch maximum establish neither
required sup norm.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\small
\caption{UPI--TRM: one fixed-base $K$-step collection and proposal}
\label{alg:upi_app}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} $x\in\XX$, $y_0\in\YY$, and
$h_0\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}$; for persistent-$z$ only, $z_0\in\ZZ$
\STATE $\gamma\in[0,1)$, $n\in\mathbb N_0$, $K\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}$,
$\alpha\in[0,1]$, and target-retention coefficient $\tau\in[0,1]$
\STATE \textbf{Recurrent mode:} either $R>0$ with
$\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}=\Pi_R$, or projection disabled with
$\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}=\operatorname{Id}_{\ZZ}$; set
$\widetilde f_\theta=\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}\circ f_\theta$
\STATE Initialize $\bar{s}_0 \gets (x,y_0,z_0,h_0)$ and collected length $\ell\gets0$
\FOR{$k = 0$ to $K-1$}
  \STATE $z_k^{(n)} \gets \widetilde f_\theta^{\circ n}(z_k,y_k,x)$ \hfill \emph{// $n$ inner steps}
  \STATE Sample $a_k \sim \pi_{\phi}(a \mid y_k,z_k^{(n)},x)$
  \STATE $y_{k+1} \gets \mathrm{edit}(y_k,a_k;x)$; receive $r_k$ and terminal flag $d_k$; set $h_{k+1}\gets h_k-1$
  \IF{$d_k$ or $h_{k+1}=0$}
    \STATE $\bar{s}_{k+1} \gets s_{\mathrm{abs}}$; store $(\bar{s}_k,a_k,r_k,s_{\mathrm{abs}})$ in replay
    \STATE $\ell\gets k+1$; \textbf{break}
  \ELSE
    \STATE $z_{k+1} \gets z_k^{(n)}$ \hfill \emph{// Carry only after a nonterminal transition}
    \STATE $\bar{s}_{k+1} \gets (x,y_{k+1},z_{k+1},h_{k+1})$
    \STATE Store $(\bar{s}_k,a_k,r_k,\bar{s}_{k+1})$ in replay; $\ell\gets k+1$
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute $G_{\mathrm{fold}}$ for the collected length $\ell$ via~\eqref{eq:G_folded_implementation}
\STATE Fit the value head $\psi\to\psi^+$; the fixed-base protocol keeps the recurrent map $\theta$ frozen
\STATE Form advantages $\widehat A$ via centered estimator (see below)
\STATE Policy-head step with $F_n$ frozen $\to$ candidate $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$
\STATE $\pi_{\mathrm{new}} \gets (1-\alpha)\pi_{\phi} + \alpha\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$
\STATE Deploy the exact pointwise mixture for the theorem; optional distillation or parameter interpolation is outside it
\STATE $\bar\psi^+ \gets \tau \bar\psi + (1-\tau)\psi^+$ \hfill \emph{// $\tau$ retains the old target}
\STATE \textbf{Output:} fitted head $\psi^+$, candidate
$\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}$, exact mixture $\pi_{\mathrm{new}}$, and updated
target head $\bar\psi^+$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The fixed-base implementation seals the base policy and recurrent map
$F_n$ before collection. Replay comes from that base; optimization changes
the policy-independent value head and one candidate policy head. Repeated
candidate optimizer steps remain internal to the same proposal. Its exact
mixture is evaluated or deployed without replacing the base.


The reference implementation stores the carried input and successor latents, the remaining-edit clock, terminal cause, terminal mask, and behavior log probability in replay. Recomputed episodic latents use the current evaluator, while persistent updates consume the recorded augmented state. On a terminal record, the environment has already folded the common absorbing tail into the reward, and every target path replaces the successor-value contribution by zero with a nonfinite-safe selection. The serialized terminal successor tensors are therefore never bootstrapped. This folded representation is return-equivalent to the single $s_{\mathrm{abs}}$ used in the proofs; it does not define puzzle-specific absorbing states.

\textbf{Advantage estimation.}
The theory uses the centered estimator~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}. The $\alpha\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}/(1-\gamma)$ term in the readable specialization~\eqref{eq:main_theorem} requires \emph{exact} statewise centering $\E_{a\sim\pi}[\widehat{A}(s,a)]=0$. This holds whenever the estimator is constructed to be exactly centered. For Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ahat_def_app}, the baseline $B_V(s)=\E_{b\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[\widehat Q_V(s,b)]$ must be evaluated exactly; the finite discrete implementation uses explicit summation. With a learned value baseline, as in standard GAE, centering is only approximate and must enter through the signed defect $c(s)$ or an upper bound $B_\alpha$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error}. GAE is not covered by the displayed advantage bridge unless its uniform estimator error is supplied separately.

\textbf{Operator-norm control and contraction-oriented details.}
The implementation can optionally apply operator-norm clamping on the inner
$z\!\to\!z$ recursion (periodic rescaling using power-iteration estimates),
followed by per-layer output scaling or an explicit scalar factor. The
fixed-base exact path rejects scheduled clamping that would mutate the frozen
recurrent map. These interventions do not certify the uniform modulus on the
declared domain in Assumption~\ref{ass:Lip}. A possible local mini-batch diagnostic is the
finite-difference ratio
\[
\hat L_z = \max_i \frac{\|f_\theta(z_i,y_i,x_i) - f_\theta(z_i',y_i,x_i)\|}{\|z_i - z_i'\|}.
\]

\textbf{Enforcing forward-invariance.}
For an invocation on $\mathcal D^\circ$, the theorem needs
$z_{\mathrm{init}}(x(s),y(s))\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{inv}}$ and forward
invariance only for represented $s\in\mathcal D^\circ$. A global
projection-enabled implementation is one sufficient way to enforce these
conditions. In projection-disabled mode,
$\mathsf P_{\mathrm{rec}}=\operatorname{Id}_{\ZZ}$ and no projection-induced
invariant ball follows; $R$ is absent rather than set to zero.
\begin{itemize}
\item With $R>0$, use an initialization $z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y)$ with $\|z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y)\|_2 \le R$, $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{inv}} = \{z : \|z\|_2 \le R\}$. In the reference implementation, the initial latent comes from learned latent seeds (optionally augmented by an initialization encoder) and is projected into the same ball.
\item After each update, apply Euclidean projection onto the ball: $z \gets \Pi_R(z)$ with $\Pi_R$ as in~\eqref{eq:projection}.
\item Under the Euclidean norm this projection is $1$-Lipschitz
(non-expansive) and therefore cannot increase an independently established
contraction modulus.
\item Equivalently, define $\widetilde f_\theta := \Pi_R \circ f_\theta$ and unroll $\widetilde f_\theta^{\circ n}$: $z^{(t+1)}=\Pi_R(f_\theta(z^{(t)},y,x))$ for $t=0,\dots,n-1$.
\end{itemize}

For persistent-$z$, one possible diagnostic is the fresh-initialization discrepancy $\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{fresh}}(n)=\max_t\|z_t^{(n)}-z_{\mathrm{init}}(x,y_t)\|$. It measures separation from a fresh initialization; it is not a fixed-point tracking bound and is not a substitute for $\Cdrift(n)$ or the augmented residual.

\textbf{Distillation.}
When distilling $\pi_{\mathrm{new}}$ to $\pi_\phi$, we minimize:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{distill}}(\phi) = \E_{\bar{s}\sim\text{buffer}}[\KL(\pi_{\mathrm{new}}(\cdot\mid\bar{s}) \| \pi_\phi(\cdot\mid\bar{s}))].
\]
This buffer-averaged objective is a heuristic diagnostic, not a uniform discrepancy certificate. The distilled policy is not the exact mixture. Corollary~\ref{cor:combined_cpi_deployment} covers it only when a uniform TV or KL discrepancy is established on the deployment domain; a finite-batch $\KL(\pi_{\mathrm{new}}\|\pi_\phi)$ does not establish that premise. Parameter interpolation is also a different policy and must not be assigned a zero realization gap from the exact pointwise mixture.

\section{Additional Exposition and Proofs}
\label{app:additional_exposition}

\subsection{Extended related work}
\label{app:extended_related_work}

\paragraph{Recurrent reasoning and finite computation.}
TRMs~\citep{TRM2025} and Hierarchical Reasoning Models~\citep{HRM2025}
use repeated latent updates for structured prediction; subsequent work
studies training speed and test-time adaptation
~\citep{Qasim2025CGAR,McGovern2025TRMAdapt}. Deep equilibrium models instead
use an equilibrium representation~\citep{Bai2019DEQ}. Our primary analysis
concerns a value head read after finitely many recurrent steps. The
fixed-point analysis appears as a specialization when uniform contraction
is established, while the finite-reference certificate also covers
transient computation.

\paragraph{Approximate policy evaluation.}
Approximate dynamic programming studies how imperfect value estimates affect
control~\citep{BertsekasTsitsiklis1996,Munos2003API,Scherrer2014}.
\citet{Munos2003API} analyzes API evaluation errors in weighted quadratic
norms. The fitted-value-iteration analysis of
\citet{MunosSzepesvari2008} includes weighted-$L_p$ performance bounds,
discounted-average concentrability, inherent Bellman residuals, and
finite-sample terms. These results track errors through update sequences.
Here we use an a posteriori residual bound for one policy and two finite
recurrent endpoints. Our primary theorem assumes population sup-norm
control; Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_lp} gives a weighted-norm version with
an explicit fixed-kernel occupancy-domination condition. The recurrence
producing the endpoints need not implement Bellman iteration.

\paragraph{Conservative policy improvement.}
CPI mixes a current policy with a candidate to limit the effect of occupancy
shift~\citep{KakadeLangford2002,Kakade2003Thesis}. Safe policy-iteration
methods develop lower bounds and update rules under different approximation
assumptions~\citep{Pirotta2013SafePI,Metelli2021SafePI,Eshwar2025MonotoneCPI};
TRPO uses a related surrogate with a trust-region update
~\citep{Schulman2015TRPO}. We analyze one exact probability mixture and
supply its evaluation-error term from the recurrent certificate. Keeping the
signed defect and the exact selector-based occupancy bound distinguishes the
roles of evaluator accuracy and policy shift. Sequence-level termination or
approximate-optimality results require the additional oracle and coverage
conditions of the corresponding CPI analyses. Table~\ref{tab:related_scope}
compares these theorem scopes.

\paragraph{Structured decisions and verifier feedback.}
Learning-to-search methods cast structured prediction as sequential decision
making~\citep{Daume2009SEARN,Ross2011DAgger,Chang2015LOLS}; checker-based
constraint tasks provide concrete edit environments
~\citep{Mehta2021SudokuRL,Klein2022SudokuRL}. Verifiable-reward reasoning
systems such as DeepSeek-R1 use automatic correctness signals for RL
~\citep{DeepSeekR1}. UPI--TRM studies such feedback in an edit MDP with an
explicit finite-depth value evaluator. Its internal recurrence is distinct
from unrolled planning over transitions~\citep{Tamar2016VIN},
trajectory search~\citep{janner2021trajectory}, and sequence-modeling
approaches to control~\citep{chen2021decision,laskin2022context}:
$n$ counts computation within an evaluation, whereas $K$ counts environment
steps in a Bellman backup.

\subsection{Finite-endpoint remarks}
\label{app:finite_endpoint_remarks}

\begin{remark}[Noncontractive finite endpoints]
\label{rem:noncontractive_endpoints}
Define
\begin{equation}
D_{n,m}:=\|U_n-U_m\|_\infty.
\label{eq:Dnm_noncontractive}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_reference_depth} and the finite-path bound remain valid
when a recurrent Lipschitz modulus is unavailable or when a valid modulus
satisfies $L_z\ge1$. Only the optional infinite-depth geometric specialization
is then lost. A small $D_{n,m}$ alone does not certify value accuracy: the
depth-$m$ Bellman residual is still required. Nor does the theorem imply that
error decreases monotonically with depth. For example, the scalar map
$F(z)=-z$ is nonconvergent away from zero, while endpoints whose depths have
the same parity agree exactly. This illustration is not a model of the learned
system; it shows that the certificate concerns only the selected endpoint
pair.

When the corresponding premises are all available, crude endpoint bounds may
be combined as
\begin{equation}
D_{n,m}
\le
\min\left\{
2V_{\max},\;
2L_VR,\;
L_VC_z\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}L_z^j
\right\}.
\label{eq:endpoint_gap_crude_bounds}
\end{equation}
The first term assumes both endpoint outputs are bounded by $V_{\max}$; the
second assumes both endpoint latents lie in $B_R$ and the head is
$L_V$-Lipschitz; the third assumes a uniform finite-path recurrent modulus
$L_z\ge0$ and first-increment bound $C_z$. If only some premises are proved,
the minimum is taken only over the corresponding available terms. For
$L_z\ge1$, the last term need not shrink with $n$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Frozen schedules and changing parameters]
\label{rem:frozen_schedule_scope}
A fixed depth-indexed schedule
$z^{(j+1)}=T_j(s,z^{(j)})$ may replace repeated use of one map in the abstract
finite-endpoint and telescoping arguments, provided the schedule is frozen at
the analyzed snapshot and produces bounded measurable $U_n,U_m$ on the same
Bellman domain. A recurrent mode or clock that changes across environment
steps must be observed and included in the Markov state. If the recurrent
maps, value head, policy, or Bellman operator change between defining the
endpoints, collecting trajectories, evaluating the residual, and deploying
the policy, the fixed-snapshot theorem does not apply as written. It is not a
theorem about SGD, TD, BPTT, or adaptive parameter updates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap}}
\label{app:proof_candidate_overlap}

\begin{proof}
Let $q_e(s,a)=\gamma\E[e(S')\mid s,a]$. Exact centering gives
$b(s)=\E_{\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}}q_e-\E_\pi q_e$. By
Definition~\ref{def:adv_closure}, there is a common action set of full
$\pi(\cdot\mid s)$- and $\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)$-measure on which
$q_e(s,a)\in[-\gamma\delta_V,\gamma\delta_V]$. The total-variation
expectation inequality on this common essential range proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:candidate_overlap_pointwise}; taking a supremum or a
$d_\pi$ expectation gives the two corollaries. For a $K$-step estimator,
$q_e^{(K)}(s,a)=\gamma^K\E[e(S_K)\mid s,a]$ has the corresponding essential
range $[-\gamma^K\delta_V,\gamma^K\delta_V]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:target_network_bridge}}
\label{app:proof_target_network_bridge}

\begin{proof}
Add and subtract the target-network population backup:
\[
U_q-\mathcal T_K^\pi U_q
=
\left[U_q-\left(r_K^\pi+\gamma^K P_\pi^K\bar V\right)\right]
+\gamma^K P_\pi^K(\bar V-U_q).
\]
The triangle inequality gives the first bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_network_residual_bridge}. Invariance makes $P_\pi^K$ a
Markov operator on $B_b(\mathcal E)$, so it is nonexpansive in the pointwise
sup norm and gives the second bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact_mixture_occupancy}}
\label{app:proof_mixture_occupancy}

\begin{proof}
Realize each mixture decision with an independent Bernoulli selector. The
event that decisions $0,\ldots,t-1$ all select the current-policy component
has probability $(1-\alpha)^t$ and, conditional on that event, produces the
current-policy state law before decision $t$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\rho P_{\pi_\alpha}^t,\rho P_\pi^t)
\le1-(1-\alpha)^t.
\]
Convexity of total variation and the geometric identity give
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{TV}(d_{\pi_\alpha},d_\pi)
&\le(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^\infty
\gamma^t[1-(1-\alpha)^t]\\
&=\frac{\gamma\alpha}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}.
\end{align*}
The exponent is $t$, not $t+1$, because occupancy is measured before
decision $t$. The formula gives zero at $\alpha=0$ or $\gamma=0$ and
$\gamma$ at $\alpha=1$, and improves the generic bound because
$1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha\ge1-\gamma$. In the countable case this gives
$2\gamma\alpha/(1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha)
\le2\gamma\alpha/(1-\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Span-based safe-step extension}
\label{app:safe_step_statement}

\begin{corollary}[Span-based safe mixture step]
\label{cor:one_step_improvement_condition}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone_with_error}, fix one measurable
common policy-pair domain $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ from
Definition~\ref{def:mixture_deployment_domains}, with
$\mathcal C_\pi\subseteq\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$. Use one fixed estimator
$\widehat A$ satisfying exact statewise centering on $\mathcal C_\pi$ for
every $\alpha\in[0,1]$, and define its fixed candidate-defect function
\begin{equation}
b(s):=
\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}
[\widehat A(s,a)-A^\pi(s,a)].
\label{eq:safe_step_candidate_defect}
\end{equation}
Neither the estimator nor this defect object depends on $\alpha$. Define,
once on $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}$,
\begin{align}
g(s)
&:=\E_{a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}[A^\pi(s,a)],
&
\Delta_g
&:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}}g(s)
-\inf_{s\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{pair}}}g(s),
\label{eq:pair_span_def}
\end{align}
and assume this function is bounded and measurable there. Let
$\overline{\beta}_d$ be any finite, $\alpha$-independent, established
one-sided bound satisfying
\begin{equation}
\overline{\beta}_d\ge \E_{s\sim d_\pi}b(s).
\label{eq:beta_d_bound}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\widehat g
:=\E_{s\sim d_\pi,\,a\sim\pi_{\mathrm{cand}}(\cdot\mid s)}
[\widehat A(s,a)],
\qquad
M_d:=\widehat g-\overline{\beta}_d.
\label{eq:Md_safe_step}
\end{equation}
Then, simultaneously for every $\alpha\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\eta(\pi_\alpha)-\eta(\pi)
\ge
\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
\left[
M_d-
\frac{\gamma\alpha\Delta_g}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}
\right].
\label{eq:safe_step_interval_bound}
\end{equation}
Thus $\eta(\pi_\alpha)\ge\eta(\pi)$ is certified whenever
\begin{equation}
M_d\ge
\frac{\gamma\alpha\Delta_g}{1-\gamma+\gamma\alpha}.
\label{eq:sufficient_improvement_condition}
\end{equation}
Three immediate choices preserve the same fixed defect object:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the exact signed defect,
$\overline{\beta}_d=\E_{d_\pi}b(s)$;
\item the occupancy-averaged absolute defect,
$\overline{\beta}_d=\E_{d_\pi}|b(s)|$;
\item the uniform specialization already used above,
$\overline{\beta}_d=\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsA0_def}.
\end{enumerate}
The third choice recovers the previous uniform statement as an immediate
specialization. Appendix~\ref{app:safe_step_boundaries} gives the exact
positive-step threshold and all boundary cases.
Under Corollaries~\ref{cor:epsA_dials} and~\ref{cor:candidate_overlap},
with the finite-reference estimator $\widehat A=\widehat A_{U_n}$, one may use
$\E_{d_\pi}|b(s)|\le
2\gamma B_{n,m}\E_{d_\pi}\tau(s)$ for the occupancy-averaged
specialization, or
$\varepsilon_{A,\mathrm{cand}}\le2\gamma B_{n,m}\tau_\infty$ for the
uniform specialization. The bound $\tau_\infty\le1$ recovers the previous
uniform fallback.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Extended discussion and certification program}
\label{app:extended_discussion}

\paragraph{Finite computation is the primary object.}
A recurrent evaluator can be analyzed at the depth where it is used. A
second finite endpoint separates the effect of additional computation from
agreement with the policy's Bellman equation. This produces a value-error
radius that feeds directly into advantage and CPI bounds. A small endpoint
gap alone is insufficient: the reference must also have a small residual.
Uniform contraction supplies a geometric specialization, but transient
recurrent dynamics remain within the primary finite-reference analysis.

\paragraph{Persistent memory changes the decision process.}
The latent and remaining edit budget determine future decisions and
termination. Including them in the state makes their role explicit.
The shared frozen map is what allows the base, candidate, and their mixture
to inhabit one augmented MDP. Increasing the comparison depth is then an
evaluation operation, whereas increasing the deployed depth can change the
transition kernel. A small Euclidean distance between two recurrent maps
alone cannot control that change (Remark~\ref{rem:shared_map_impossibility}).

\paragraph{What learned-model certification would require.}
A numerical certificate needs uniform residual control on the relevant
successor domain, exact centering or a justified signed-defect bound, and
control of any discrepancy between the deployed policy and the exact
mixture. A target-network residual also needs its propagated lag.
Finite-population maxima and regression losses are useful diagnostics, but
they do not establish those uniform premises. Exact action enumeration
resolves the action expectation, not state or latent coverage. The
weighted-norm alternative makes that coverage requirement explicit.

Projection can bound the latent set without making these quantities easy to
certify or small enough to be useful. It does not bound an arbitrary external
state space, and products of global Lipschitz bounds or whole-ball interval
bounds can be loose. A possible next step is to enumerate finite external
states and actions, construct reachable latent enclosures, and preserve
shared inputs and weights when bounding $U_n-U_m$. The same computation must
also certify rewards, masks, successors, and Bellman expectations.
Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} records the implementation details needed
for that connection; such a learned-model verifier is not established here.

\paragraph{Scope and open questions.}
The results concern one frozen proposal. They do not establish convergence of
its optimizer or a recursively promoted policy sequence, finite-sample
coverage, near-optimality, or improvement on a learned task. The exact MDP
calculation shows that the certificates can be informative and that retaining
signed error can matter. Evaluating their diagnostic value for trained
recurrent models, and obtaining practical uniform certificates, remain
separate empirical and verification questions.
